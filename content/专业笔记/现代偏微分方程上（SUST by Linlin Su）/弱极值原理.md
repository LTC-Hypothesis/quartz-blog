# Weak Maximum Principle (WMP)

## 0. Motivation and notation

In one dimension, if

$$
u\in C^2(a,b)\cap C([a,b]),\qquad u''\ge0\text{ in }(a,b),
$$

then convexity gives

$$
\max_{[a,b]}u=\max_{\partial(a,b)}u=\max\{u(a),u(b)\}.
$$

**Question.** For $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, does $Lu\ge0$ imply that the maximum is attained on $\partial\Omega$?

Throughout, use the positive-principal-part convention from [[Intro 二阶PDE#1. Second-order elliptic equations|Intro]]:

$$
Lu=a_{ij}(x)u_{x_i x_j}+b_i(x)u_{x_i}+c(x)u,
\qquad
L_0u=a_{ij}(x)u_{x_i x_j}+b_i(x)u_{x_i}.
$$

The basic assumptions are:

- **(A1)** $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is open and bounded.
- **(A2)** $A=(a_{ij})$ is symmetric and $L$ is strictly elliptic:
  $$
  a_{ij}(x)\xi_i\xi_j\ge\lambda_0|\xi|^2
  \quad\forall x\in\Omega,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^n
  $$
  for some $\lambda_0>0$.
- **(A3)** $|b_i(x)|\le M$ in $\Omega$, for $i=1,\ldots,n$.

These WMP statements do not require smoothness of $\partial\Omega$. Connectedness is needed later for the [[强极值原理#3. Strong Maximum Principle|SMP]].

## 1. WMP with c identically zero

> [!thm] Thm 1 · WMP with $c\equiv0$
> Assume **(A1)–(A3)** and $c\equiv0$. If
> $$
> u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega),\qquad Lu\ge0\text{ in }\Omega,
> $$
> then
> $$
> \boxed{\max_{\overline\Omega}u=\max_{\partial\Omega}u.}
> $$

**Proof.**

**Step 1: $Lu>0$ in $\Omega$.** Since $\overline\Omega$ is compact, $u$ attains a maximum at some $x_0\in\overline\Omega$.

If $x_0\in\Omega$, then

$$
Du(x_0)=0,\qquad D^2u(x_0)\le0.
$$

As $A(x_0)$ is positive definite,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lu(x_0)
&=a_{ij}(x_0)u_{x_i x_j}(x_0)\\
&=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(A(x_0)^{1/2}D^2u(x_0)A(x_0)^{1/2}\bigr)
\le0,
\end{aligned}
$$

contradicting $Lu>0$. Thus a maximum occurs on $\partial\Omega$.

**Step 2: $Lu\ge0$ in $\Omega$.** For $\varepsilon>0$, define

$$
v_\varepsilon(x)=u(x)+\varepsilon e^{\alpha x_1},
$$

where $\alpha>0$ will be chosen. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lv_\varepsilon
&=Lu+\varepsilon(\alpha^2a_{11}+\alpha b_1)e^{\alpha x_1}\\
&\ge\varepsilon(\alpha^2\lambda_0-M\alpha)e^{\alpha x_1}>0
\end{aligned}
$$

if $\alpha>M/\lambda_0$. By Step 1,

$$
\max_{\overline\Omega}v_\varepsilon
=\max_{\partial\Omega}v_\varepsilon.
$$

Since $\Omega$ is bounded, $v_\varepsilon\to u$ uniformly on $\overline\Omega$ as $\varepsilon\to0^+$. Passing to the limit proves the claim. **QED**

> [!remark]
> 1. The exponential perturbation only needs one coefficient $b_i$ to be bounded below, together with the lower ellipticity bound in that coordinate direction.
> 2. $Lu\ge0$ means that $u$ is a lower solution. It does not imply convexity in several dimensions: $u(x,y)=2x^2-y^2$ has $\Delta u=2>0$ but an indefinite Hessian.
> 3. Applying the theorem to $-u$ yields the **weak minimum principle**:
>    $$
>    Lu\le0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
>    \min_{\overline\Omega}u=\min_{\partial\Omega}u.
>    $$
> 4. If $Lu=0$, both principles apply, so
>    $$
>    \max_{\overline\Omega}|u|=\max_{\partial\Omega}|u|.
>    $$

### Physical interpretation

For the stationary heat equation

$$
-Lu=f(x),\qquad Lu=\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u),
$$

the sign $Lu\ge0$ corresponds to $f\le0$: a heat sink (“fridge”). The opposite sign corresponds to a heat source (“oven”). The [[Intro 二阶PDE#4. Heat equation and steady states|heat equation derivation]] explains this sign convention.

## 2. WMP with c nonpositive

Use the manuscript's convention

$$
u^+=\max\{u,0\},\qquad
u^-=\min\{u,0\},\qquad u=u^++u^-.
$$

Thus $u^-\le0$ here.

> [!thm] Thm 2 · WMP with $c\le0$
> Assume **(A1)–(A3)** and $c\le0$ in $\Omega$. If
> $$
> u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega),\qquad Lu\ge0\text{ in }\Omega,
> $$
> then
> $$
> \boxed{\max_{\overline\Omega}u\le\max_{\partial\Omega}u^+.}
> $$
> If $\max_{\overline\Omega}u>0$, then in fact
> $$
> \max_{\overline\Omega}u=\max_{\partial\Omega}u.
> $$

**Proof.** Let

$$
\Omega^+=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>0\}.
$$

If $\Omega^+=\varnothing$, then $u\le0$ on $\overline\Omega$ and the result follows. Otherwise, on $\Omega^+$,

$$
L_0u=Lu-cu\ge0.
$$

The [[#1. WMP with c identically zero|WMP for $c\equiv0$]] gives

$$
\max_{\overline{\Omega^+}}u=\max_{\partial\Omega^+}u>0.
$$

At every point of $\partial\Omega^+\cap\Omega$, continuity implies $u=0$. Hence a positive maximum must occur on $\partial\Omega^+\cap\partial\Omega$, which proves the assertion. **QED**

### Why the sign and the positive part matter

**Counterexample if $c\le0$ is dropped.** On $(0,\pi)$, let

$$
Lu=u''+u,\qquad u(x)=\sin x.
$$

Then $Lu=0$ and $u=0$ at the endpoints, but $\max u=1>0$.

**The positive part cannot be replaced by $u$.** On $(-1,1)$, take

$$
Lu=u''-u,\qquad u(x)=-(x^2+100).
$$

Then

$$
Lu=x^2+98>0,\qquad
\max_{[-1,1]}u=-100>-101=\max_{\partial(-1,1)}u.
$$

The theorem instead gives $\max u\le0$, which is correct. This example also shows why its inequality cannot in general be replaced by equality.

> [!remark] Minimum and absolute-value versions · EX
> Under the same hypotheses:
> $$
> Lu\le0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> \min_{\overline\Omega}u\ge\min_{\partial\Omega}u^-.
> $$
> If $Lu=0$, applying both estimates gives
> $$
> \max_{\overline\Omega}|u|=\max_{\partial\Omega}|u|.
> $$

## 3. Comparison and uniqueness

> [!thm] Thm 3 · Comparison Principle
> Assume **(A1)–(A3)** and $c\le0$. If $u,v\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$ satisfy
> $$
> \begin{cases}
> Lu\le Lv&\text{in }\Omega,\\
> u\ge v&\text{on }\partial\Omega,
> \end{cases}
> $$
> then $u\ge v$ in $\overline\Omega$.

**Proof.** Put $w=v-u$. Then $Lw\ge0$ in $\Omega$ and $w\le0$ on $\partial\Omega$. Thus

$$
\max_{\overline\Omega}w\le\max_{\partial\Omega}w^+=0.
$$

Hence $v\le u$. **QED**

> [!thm] Cor 1 · Uniqueness for the Dirichlet B.V.P.
> Under the same hypotheses, the problem
> $$
> \begin{cases}
> Lu=f(x)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
> u=\varphi(x)&\text{on }\partial\Omega
> \end{cases}
> $$
> has at most one solution in $C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$.

**Proof.** If $u_1,u_2$ are solutions, then

$$
L(u_1-u_2)=0\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad
u_1-u_2=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.
$$

Apply comparison in both directions. **QED**

### Semilinear Dirichlet problem

> [!thm] Application · EX
> Let $\Omega$ be bounded and $f\in C^1(\mathbb{R})$ with $f'(s)\le0$ for every $s\in\mathbb{R}$. Then
> $$
> \begin{cases}
> \Delta u+f(u)=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
> u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega
> \end{cases}
> $$
> has at most one solution in $C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$.

**Proof.** For two solutions $u_1,u_2$, put $w=u_1-u_2$. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

$$
f(u_1(x))-f(u_2(x))=c(x)w(x),
$$

where

$$
c(x)=\int_0^1 f'\bigl(u_2(x)+t(u_1(x)-u_2(x))\bigr)\,dt\le0.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\Delta w+c(x)w=0\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad w=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.
$$

The WMP applied to $w$ and $-w$ gives $w=0$. **QED**

> [!remark]
> The manuscript writes $c(x)=f'(\xi(x))$ using the mean value theorem. The integral expression above is the same linearization and specifies the coefficient directly.

## 4. A priori estimate and stability

> [!thm] Supremum estimate
> Assume **(A1)–(A3)** and $c\le0$. If $u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$ satisfies $Lu=f$ in $\Omega$, then
> $$
> \boxed{
> \max_{\overline\Omega}|u|
> \le\max_{\partial\Omega}|u|+K\sup_\Omega|f|,
> }
> $$
> where $K$ depends only on the domain and the ellipticity/drift bounds, not on $u$ or $f$.

**Proof.** If $\sup_\Omega|f|=\infty$, the assertion is vacuous. Otherwise set

$$
F=\sup_\Omega|f|,\qquad
B=\max_{\partial\Omega}|u|,\qquad
d=\max_{x\in\overline\Omega}|x|.
$$

Choose $\alpha>0$ so that

$$
\lambda_0\alpha^2-M\alpha\ge1,
$$

and define the barrier

$$
\overline u(x)=B+\bigl(e^{2\alpha d}-e^{\alpha(x_1+d)}\bigr)F.
$$

Since $-d\le x_1\le d$, we have $\overline u\ge B\ge0$ and $e^{\alpha(x_1+d)}\ge1$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
L\overline u
&=-(\alpha^2a_{11}+\alpha b_1)e^{\alpha(x_1+d)}F+c\overline u\\
&\le-(\lambda_0\alpha^2-M\alpha)e^{\alpha(x_1+d)}F\\
&\le-F.
\end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand,

$$
L(\pm u)=\pm f\ge-F\ge L\overline u,
\qquad \pm u\le B\le\overline u\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.
$$

The [[#3. Comparison and uniqueness|comparison principle]] gives $\pm u\le\overline u$. Hence

$$
\max_{\overline\Omega}|u|
\le B+e^{2\alpha d}F.
$$

One may take $K=e^{2\alpha d}$. **QED**

### Structural stability

Suppose the same operator $L$ is used in

$$
\begin{cases}Lu=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\u=\varphi&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}
\qquad
\begin{cases}Lu_\alpha=f_\alpha&\text{in }\Omega,\\u_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}
$$

Applying the estimate to $u-u_\alpha$ yields

$$
\max_{\overline\Omega}|u-u_\alpha|
\le\max_{\partial\Omega}|\varphi-\varphi_\alpha|
+K\sup_\Omega|f-f_\alpha|.
$$

Thus uniform approximation of the forcing and boundary data gives uniform approximation of the solutions, when those solutions exist. The estimate itself is not an existence theorem.

## 5. WMP on unbounded domains

### Why infinity must be included

**Example 1.** Let

$$
\Omega=\{(x,y):y>0\},\qquad u(x,y)=(1+x^2)y.
$$

Then $\Delta u=2y>0$ in $\Omega$ and $u=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, while $\sup_\Omega u=\infty$. The bounded-domain WMP cannot simply be reused.

**Example 2.** On $\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\overline{B_1(0)}$,

$$
u(x)=\log|x|,\qquad \Delta u=0,\qquad
u|_{\partial B_1(0)}=0,\qquad \sup_\Omega u=\infty.
$$

> [!thm] WMP with a boundary value at infinity
> Let $\Omega$ be unbounded. For every sufficiently large $R$, assume strict ellipticity and bounded drift coefficients on the truncated domain $\Omega\cap B_R(0)$; the bounds may depend on $R$.
> Let $u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$ satisfy $Lu\ge0$, and set
> $$
> u(\infty)=\limsup_{\substack{x\in\Omega\\|x|\to\infty}}u(x).
> $$
> If $c\equiv0$, then
> $$
> \boxed{\sup_\Omega u=\max\left\{\sup_{\partial\Omega}u,\ u(\infty)\right\}.}
> $$
> If $c\le0$ and $\partial\Omega\ne\varnothing$, then
> $$
> \boxed{\sup_\Omega u\le\max\left\{\sup_{\partial\Omega}u^+,\ u(\infty)\right\}.}
> $$
> A version also covering empty boundary is
> $$
> \sup_\Omega u\le\max\left\{0,\sup_{\partial\Omega}u,\ u(\infty)\right\},
> $$
> with $\sup\varnothing=-\infty$.

**Proof.** For $c\equiv0$, apply the bounded-domain WMP to

$$
\Omega_R=\Omega\cap B_R(0).
$$

Its boundary is contained in the union of the original boundary and the artificial spherical boundary. Thus, for $x\in\Omega_R$,

$$
u(x)\le\max\left\{
\sup_{\partial\Omega\cap\overline{B_R}}u,
\sup_{\overline\Omega\cap\partial B_R}u
\right\}.
$$

Let $R\to\infty$. The second term is controlled by $u(\infty)$; boundary values can also be approximated from within $\Omega$ by continuity. This proves the upper bound. Both $\sup_{\partial\Omega}u$ and $u(\infty)$ are at most $\sup_\Omega u$, so equality follows.

For $c\le0$, use the positive-part WMP on $\Omega_R$ and pass to the limit in the same way. **QED**

> [!remark] 整理注
> In the statement on p. 2, the manuscript writes strict ellipticity on $\Omega\setminus B_R(0)$, but its proof applies the bounded-domain theorem on $\Omega\cap B_R(0)$. The hypothesis above is stated on the truncated domains required by that proof. The limit at infinity is a **limsup**, not necessarily an existing ordinary limit.

## 6. Harmonic functions in an exterior domain

> [!thm] Exterior decay estimate
> Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded domain, $n\ge3$, and write $E=\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\overline\Omega$. Suppose
> $$
> u\in C^2(E)\cap C(\overline E),\qquad
> \Delta u=0\text{ in }E,\qquad
> \lim_{\substack{x\in E\\|x|\to\infty}}u(x)=0.
> $$
> Then
> $$
> |u(x)|=O(|x|^{2-n})\qquad(|x|\to\infty).
> $$
> More precisely, for any fixed $x_0\in\Omega$ there is $M>0$ such that
> $$
> |u(x)|\le M|x-x_0|^{2-n}\qquad(x\in E).
> $$

**Proof.** Let

$$
\Gamma(x)=|x|^{2-n}.
$$

A direct radial calculation gives $\Delta\Gamma=0$ for $x\ne0$ (**EX** in the manuscript). Choose $x_0\in\Omega$ and $M>0$ large enough that

$$
M\Gamma(x-x_0)\ge|u(x)|\qquad(x\in\partial E).
$$

For each sign, define

$$
v_\pm(x)=M\Gamma(x-x_0)\pm u(x).
$$

Then $\Delta v_\pm=0$ in $E$, $v_\pm\ge0$ on $\partial E$ and $v_\pm\to0$ at infinity. The minimum principle on the exterior component, and the bounded-domain minimum principle on any bounded components, imply $v_\pm\ge0$. Therefore

$$
|u(x)|\le M|x-x_0|^{2-n}.
$$

For sufficiently large $|x|$, $|x-x_0|\ge|x|/2$, giving the claimed decay. **QED**

> [!remark] Positive boundary values
> If $u>0$ everywhere on $\partial E$, compactness and continuity allow a sufficiently small $\widetilde M>0$ such that
> $$
> \widetilde M\Gamma(x-x_0)\le u(x)\qquad(x\in\partial E).
> $$
> Applying the minimum principle to $u-\widetilde M\Gamma(x-x_0)$ yields
> $$
> \widetilde M|x-x_0|^{2-n}\le u(x)\le M|x-x_0|^{2-n}.
> $$
> Consequently there are $\widetilde K,K,R_0>0$ such that
> $$
> \frac{\widetilde K}{|x|^{n-2}}\le u(x)\le\frac{K}{|x|^{n-2}}
> \qquad(x\in E,\ |x|\ge R_0).
> $$

> [!remark] 整理注
> The manuscript uses $\Omega^c$ for the exterior region. Here the open set $E=\mathbb{R}^n\setminus\overline\Omega$ is specified, and continuity up to its boundary is included because the comparison proof uses boundary values. The estimate with the shifted distance $|x-x_0|$ is valid throughout $E$; the unshifted two-sided estimate is stated for large $|x|$, so it does not introduce a spurious singularity at an exterior origin.
