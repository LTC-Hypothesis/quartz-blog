# Strong Maximum Principle (SMP)
## 0. Motivation

For a convex function $u$ on an interval, if

$$
u''\ge0\text{ in }(a,b),\qquad
u(x_0)=\max_{[a,b]}u\quad\text{for some }x_0\in(a,b),
$$

then $u\equiv u(x_0)$. The strong maximum principle is the corresponding rigidity statement for elliptic equations in a connected domain.

As in [[Intro 二阶PDE#1. Second-order elliptic equations|Intro]], write

$$
Lu=a_{ij}(x)u_{x_i x_j}+b_i(x)u_{x_i}+c(x)u,
$$

with $A=(a_{ij})$ symmetric. Strict ellipticity means

$$
a_{ij}(x)\xi_i\xi_j\ge\lambda_0|\xi|^2,
\qquad\lambda_0>0.
$$

Unlike the simplest WMP, the barrier argument below also uses an upper bound for the principal coefficients.

## 1. Interior sphere condition

> [!def] Interior sphere condition
> A domain $\Omega$ satisfies the **interior sphere condition** at $x_0\in\partial\Omega$ if there exists an open ball $B_R(y)\subset\Omega$ such that
> $$
> x_0\in\partial B_R(y).
> $$
> The ball is tangent to $\partial\Omega$ from the inside at $x_0$.

A corner of a square need not satisfy this condition, although points on the open sides do. See the sketch in [[PDE Note Week2 LectureWed.pdf#page=3|Week 2, p. 3]].

### Smooth boundary and flattening

> [!def] Boundary of class $C^m$
> The boundary $\partial\Omega$ is $C^m$ if, near every $P\in\partial\Omega$, one can translate and rotate coordinates so that $P=0$ and, in a neighborhood $N$,
> $$
> \partial\Omega\cap N=\{(x',x_n)\in N:x_n=\varphi(x')\},
> $$
> $$
> \Omega\cap N=\{(x',x_n)\in N:x_n>\varphi(x')\},
> $$
> where $x'=(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})$ and $\varphi$ is $C^m$ near $0'$.

The change of variables

$$
\Phi(x',x_n)=(x',x_n-\varphi(x'))
$$

flattens the boundary to $y_n=0$ and maps the local domain to $y_n>0$. Its inverse is

$$
\Phi^{-1}(y',y_n)=(y',y_n+\varphi(y')).
$$

This is also the local geometry used in [[4. Global Regularity|boundary regularity theory]] in the second part of the course.

> [!lemma] A $C^2$ boundary has interior tangent balls
> If $\partial\Omega$ is $C^2$, then $\Omega$ satisfies the interior sphere condition at every point of $\partial\Omega$.

**Proof.** Fix $P\in\partial\Omega$. After translation and rotation, assume $P=0$ and that the positive $x_n$-axis is the inward normal. Then

$$
\varphi(0')=0,\qquad D\varphi(0')=0.
$$

Taylor's theorem yields, near $0'$,

$$
\varphi(x')
=\frac12(x')^TD^2\varphi(0')x'+o(|x'|^2)
\le C|x'|^2
$$

for a suitable $C>0$. Consider the ball

$$
B_R(Re_n)
=\{(x',x_n):|x'|^2+(x_n-R)^2<R^2\}.
$$

Every point of the ball satisfies

$$
|x'|^2+x_n^2<2Rx_n,
\qquad
x_n>\frac{|x'|^2}{2R}.
$$

Choose $R$ sufficiently small that $1/(2R)>C$ and the ball stays in the coordinate neighborhood. Then $x_n>\varphi(x')$ throughout the ball, so $B_R(Re_n)\subset\Omega$, while $0\in\partial B_R(Re_n)$. **QED**

> [!remark]
> A $C^1$ boundary alone does not imply the interior sphere condition. The quadratic control above is the point used in this proof.

## 2. Hopf boundary point lemma

Source: [[PDE Note Week3 LectureMon.pdf#page=2|Week 3, pp. 2–3]] and [[PDE Note Week5 LectureSat.pdf#page=1|Week 5, p. 1]].

> [!lemma] Hopf boundary point lemma
> Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a domain, not necessarily bounded, and let $x_0\in\partial\Omega$. Assume, near $x_0$:
>
> - $L$ is strictly elliptic and $|a_{ij}|,|b_i|,|c|\le M$;
> - $u\in C^2(\Omega)$, $Lu\ge0$, and $u$ is continuous at $x_0$;
> - the interior sphere condition holds at $x_0$, with ball $B_R(y)$;
> - $u(x)<u(x_0)$ for interior points $x$ sufficiently close to $x_0$.
>
> Suppose one of the following holds:
> 1. $c\equiv0$;
> 2. $c\le0$ and $u(x_0)\ge0$;
> 3. $u(x_0)=0$, with no sign restriction on $c$.
>
> Then, for any outward direction $\nu$ with
> $$
> (x_0-y)\cdot\nu>0,
> $$
> the outward directional derivative, if it exists, satisfies
> $$
> \boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x_0)>0.}
> $$

The derivative is understood from the domain side:

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x_0)
=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{u(x_0)-u(x_0-t\nu)}{t}.
$$

When the boundary is smooth and $\nu$ is its outward unit normal, this is the usual normal derivative. A smaller tangent ball can be used so that all hypotheses hold in its neighborhood.

### Proof: comparison with an exponential barrier

**Step 1: construct the barrier.** Fix $0<\rho<R$, write

$$
D=B_R(y)\setminus\overline{B_\rho(y)},
\qquad
v(x)=e^{-\alpha|x-y|^2}-e^{-\alpha R^2},
$$

and choose $\alpha>0$ later. Then $v>0$ in $D$, $v=0$ on $\partial B_R(y)$, and

$$
v_{x_i}=-2\alpha(x_i-y_i)e^{-\alpha|x-y|^2},
$$

$$
v_{x_i x_j}
=\left[4\alpha^2(x_i-y_i)(x_j-y_j)-2\alpha\delta_{ij}\right]
e^{-\alpha|x-y|^2}.
$$

In particular,

$$
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}(x_0)
=-2\alpha e^{-\alpha R^2}(x_0-y)\cdot\nu<0.
$$

For $z=x-y$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lv
={}&e^{-\alpha|z|^2}
\left[4\alpha^2a_{ij}z_i z_j-2\alpha\sum_i a_{ii}
-2\alpha b\cdot z\right]\\
&+c(x)\left[e^{-\alpha|z|^2}-e^{-\alpha R^2}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\rho<|z|<R$, $|b|\le\sqrt n M$, and

$$
c(x)\left(1-e^{-\alpha(R^2-|z|^2)}\right)\ge-M,
$$

we obtain

$$
Lv\ge e^{-\alpha|z|^2}
\left[4\alpha^2\lambda_0\rho^2
-2\alpha nM-2\alpha\sqrt n MR-M\right]>0
$$

for sufficiently large $\alpha$.

**Step 2: cases (1) and (2).** Define

$$
w(x)=u(x)-u(x_0)+\varepsilon v(x).
$$

Then

$$
Lw=Lu-cu(x_0)+\varepsilon Lv\ge0\qquad\text{in }D.
$$

On the outer sphere, $w=u-u(x_0)\le0$. On the inner sphere, compactness and the strict interior inequality imply

$$
\delta=u(x_0)-\max_{\partial B_\rho(y)}u>0.
$$

Choose $\varepsilon>0$ small enough that $\varepsilon\max_{\partial B_\rho}v<\delta$. Thus $w\le0$ on all of $\partial D$. By the [[弱极值原理#2. WMP with c nonpositive|WMP]],

$$
w\le0\text{ in }\overline D,\qquad w(x_0)=0.
$$

It follows that

$$
0\le\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}(x_0)
=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x_0)
+\varepsilon\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}(x_0).
$$

Since $\partial_\nu v(x_0)<0$, we conclude $\partial_\nu u(x_0)>0$.

**Step 3: case (3).** Here $u(x_0)=0$ and $u<0$ in the tangent ball. Set

$$
c^+=\max\{c,0\},\qquad c^-=\min\{c,0\},
$$

and introduce

$$
\widetilde Lu=a_{ij}u_{x_i x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}+c^-u.
$$

Then

$$
\widetilde Lu=Lu-c^+u\ge0.
$$

The new zero-order coefficient is nonpositive, so case (2) applies to $\widetilde L$. **QED**

> [!remark] Minimum version and locality
> If $Lu\le0$ and $x_0$ is a strict boundary minimum relative to the nearby interior, apply the lemma to $-u$:
> $$
> \frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}(x_0)<0.
> $$
> In case (2), the corresponding sign condition is $u(x_0)\le0$. All operator and geometric hypotheses need only hold near the point in question.

## 3. Strong Maximum Principle

Source: [[PDE Note Week5 LectureSat.pdf#page=1|Week 5, pp. 1–2]].

> [!thm] Strong Maximum Principle
> Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bounded domain. Assume $L$ is strictly elliptic and its coefficients satisfy
> $$
> |a_{ij}|,|b_i|,|c|\le M\quad\text{in }\Omega.
> $$
> Suppose $u\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$, $Lu\ge0$, and
> $$
> u(x_0)=\max_{\overline\Omega}u
> \qquad\text{for some }x_0\in\Omega.
> $$
> If one of the following holds:
> 1. $c\equiv0$;
> 2. $c\le0$ and $u(x_0)\ge0$;
> 3. $u(x_0)=0$, irrespective of the sign of $c$;
>
> then $u\equiv u(x_0)$ in $\Omega$.

**Proof.** Let $m=u(x_0)$ and

$$
\Omega^- =\{x\in\Omega:u(x)<m\}.
$$

This is open. We show that it is empty. Suppose instead that $\Omega^-\ne\varnothing$.

**Step 1: $\partial\Omega^-\cap\Omega\ne\varnothing$.** If this intersection were empty, $\Omega^-$ would be both open and closed relative to $\Omega$. Since $x_0\notin\Omega^-$, this would separate the connected set $\Omega$ into two nonempty relatively open pieces, a contradiction.

**Step 2: find a tangent ball inside $\Omega$.** Choose $x_1\in\Omega^-$ sufficiently close to a point of $\partial\Omega^-\cap\Omega$ that

$$
\operatorname{dist}(x_1,\Omega\setminus\Omega^-)
<\operatorname{dist}(x_1,\partial\Omega).
$$

With $r=\operatorname{dist}(x_1,\Omega\setminus\Omega^-)$, the ball $B_r(x_1)$ lies in $\Omega^-$, its closure lies in $\Omega$, and its boundary touches $\Omega\setminus\Omega^-$ at a point $x_2$. Thus

$$
u<m\text{ in }B_r(x_1),\qquad u(x_2)=m.
$$

**Step 3: apply Hopf.** The [[#2. Hopf boundary point lemma|Hopf lemma]] on this ball gives an outward derivative

$$
\partial_\nu u(x_2)>0.
$$

However, $x_2\in\Omega$ is a maximum point of the $C^2$ function $u$, so $Du(x_2)=0$. This contradiction proves $\Omega^-=\varnothing$. **QED**

> [!remark]
> The ball used in this proof is inside $\Omega$; the SMP does not require an interior sphere condition on the original boundary $\partial\Omega$. Connectedness is essential. The same proof is local: on a connected domain with locally bounded coefficients and local uniform ellipticity, an attained global maximum has the same consequence under the stated sign conditions.

## 4. Separation of solutions

Source: [[PDE Note Week5 LectureSat.pdf#page=2|Week 5, p. 2]].

> [!thm] Separation of semilinear solutions
> Let $\Omega$ be a bounded domain and
> $$
> Lu=a_{ij}(x)u_{x_i x_j}+b_i(x)u_{x_i},
> $$
> with strict ellipticity and bounded coefficients. Suppose $u_1,u_2\in C^2(\Omega)$ solve
> $$
> Lu_i=f(x,u_i),\qquad i=1,2.
> $$
> Assume $f$ is continuously differentiable in its second variable and, for each $M_0>0$, $f_s(x,s)$ is uniformly bounded for $x\in\Omega$ and $|s|\le M_0$.
> If
> $$
> u_1\ge u_2\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad
> u_1(x_0)=u_2(x_0)\text{ for some }x_0\in\Omega,
> $$
> then $u_1\equiv u_2$ in $\Omega$.

**Proof.** Set $w=u_1-u_2\ge0$ and

$$
g(x,t)=f\bigl(x,tu_1(x)+(1-t)u_2(x)\bigr).
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lw
&=f(x,u_1)-f(x,u_2)\\
&=\int_0^1\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}(x,t)\,dt\\
&=w(x)\int_0^1 f_s\bigl(x,tu_1(x)+(1-t)u_2(x)\bigr)\,dt\\
&=:q(x)w(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

The function $q$ is bounded on every compact subset of $\Omega$, since $u_1,u_2$ are bounded there. Thus

$$
(L-q)w=0.
$$

To handle the possible absence of boundary bounds on $u_i$, consider

$$
Z=\{x\in\Omega:w(x)=0\}.
$$

It is nonempty and relatively closed. At any $z\in Z$, choose a ball $B\Subset\Omega$ centered at $z$. On this ball, the coefficients of $L-q$ are bounded. The function $-w\le0$ has maximum $0$ at $z$. Case (3) of the SMP gives $w\equiv0$ on $B$. Hence $Z$ is also relatively open. Connectedness implies $Z=\Omega$. **QED**

> [!remark] 整理注
> The manuscript localizes to $\Omega_\varepsilon=\{x\in\Omega:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\}$ and then applies the SMP. Such a set need not be connected. The local-ball and open–closed argument above supplies the connectedness step without assuming that all $\Omega_\varepsilon$ are connected.

Therefore two ordered solutions either coincide everywhere or are strictly separated:

$$
u_1\equiv u_2\qquad\text{or}\qquad u_1>u_2\text{ throughout }\Omega.
$$

No sign condition on $f_s$ is needed here. By contrast, the [[弱极值原理#Semilinear Dirichlet problem|Dirichlet uniqueness argument from WMP]] uses $f'\le0$ and does not assume that the two solutions are already ordered.

## 5. Comparison with Robin boundary conditions

Source: [[PDE Note Week5 LectureSat.pdf#page=3|Week 5, p. 3]].

> [!thm] Robin comparison principle
> Let $\Omega$ be a bounded domain with $C^1$ boundary, satisfying the interior sphere condition at every boundary point. Let $\nu$ denote the outward unit normal. Assume $L$ is strictly elliptic with bounded coefficients, $c\le0$, and
> $$
> \beta\ge0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\qquad
> \beta\not\equiv0.
> $$
> If $u,v\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^1(\overline\Omega)$ satisfy
> $$
> \begin{cases}
> Lu\ge Lv&\text{in }\Omega,\\
> \partial_\nu u+\beta u\le\partial_\nu v+\beta v
> &\text{on }\partial\Omega,
> \end{cases}
> $$
> then either $u<v$ everywhere on $\overline\Omega$, or $u\equiv v$.

**Proof.** Put $w=u-v$. Then

$$
Lw\ge0\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad
\partial_\nu w+\beta w\le0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.
$$

Let $m=\max_{\overline\Omega}w$. If $m<0$, then $u<v$ on $\overline\Omega$. Now assume $m\ge0$.

If $w$ attains $m$ in $\Omega$, the SMP gives $w\equiv m$. The boundary inequality then reads

$$
\beta(x)m\le0.
$$

Because $\beta\ge0$ and is positive somewhere, $m=0$, so $u\equiv v$.

Otherwise $w<m$ at every interior point, and the maximum occurs at some $x_0\in\partial\Omega$. The Hopf lemma gives

$$
\partial_\nu w(x_0)>0.
$$

But the boundary inequality gives

$$
\partial_\nu w(x_0)\le-\beta(x_0)m\le0,
$$

a contradiction. **QED**

> [!remark] 整理注 · boundary hypotheses
> The manuscript mentions the interior sphere condition at $x_0$, although the maximum point is chosen only in the proof, and lists $C(\overline\Omega)$ regularity while using normal derivatives. The statement above explicitly assumes interior tangent balls at every possible boundary maximum and $C^1$ boundary/solution regularity so that the displayed normal derivatives are defined. A $C^2$ boundary is a sufficient geometric condition by [[#1. Interior sphere condition]].

### Uniqueness for the Robin B.V.P.

Under these hypotheses,

$$
\begin{cases}
Lu=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_\nu u+\beta u=g&\text{on }\partial\Omega
\end{cases}
$$

has at most one solution in the stated class. Apply the comparison principle to two solutions in both orders.

### Dirichlet and Neumann comparisons

The Dirichlet comparison principle from [[弱极值原理#3. Comparison and uniqueness|WMP]] says

$$
Lu\ge Lv\text{ in }\Omega,\quad
u\le v\text{ on }\partial\Omega
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\le v\text{ in }\overline\Omega.
$$

The manuscript describes this informally as the limit “$\beta\to\infty$.” This is a boundary-condition analogy, not a convergence theorem established here.

If $\beta\equiv0$, the Robin condition becomes a Neumann condition. Uniqueness can fail: for the Laplacian,

$$
\Delta u=\Delta v=0\text{ in }\Omega,\qquad
\partial_\nu u=\partial_\nu v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,
$$

and $u-v$ may be any constant. This explains the role of $\beta\not\equiv0$ in the theorem.
