# Global Regularity

The target estimate is
$$u\in H^2(\Omega),\qquad\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\le C(\|u\|_2+\|f\|_2)$$
for a weak sol $u\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ of $Lu=f$, with $a_{ij}\in C^1(\overline\Omega)$, $b_i,c\in L^\infty$ and $\partial\Omega\in C^2$. Its proof is in [[5. Global H2-Regularity Theory]].

## Boundary charts

> [!def] $C^m$ boundary
> $\partial\Omega$ is $C^m$ if, near every $P\in\partial\Omega$, a translation and rotation put $P$ at the origin and give
> $$\partial\Omega\cap N=\{x_n=\varphi(x')\}\cap N,\qquad
> \Omega\cap N=\{x_n>\varphi(x')\}\cap N,$$
> where $\varphi$ is $C^m$ and $x'=(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1})$.

Flatten the boundary by
$$\Phi(x)=(x',x_n-\varphi(x'))=y,\qquad
D\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&0\\-D\varphi&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det D\Phi=1.$$
Then $\Phi$ and $\Phi^{-1}$ are $C^m$, the boundary becomes $y_n=0$, and the domain lies on the $y_n>0$ side. Since $\partial\Omega$ is compact, finitely many charts form an atlas.

## Change of variables in Sobolev spaces

2026.3.25-4.1

Let $\Omega^+=B_r(x_0)\cap\Omega$, $N^+=\Phi(\Omega^+)$ and $v(y)=u(\Phi^{-1}(y))$. On a fixed chart with bounded derivatives of $\Phi$ and its inverse:

1. Lebesgue measurability is preserved. A $C^1$ diffeomorphism and its inverse are locally Lipschitz and preserve null sets locally.
2. Since $\det D\Phi=1$,
   $$\int_{N^+}|v(y)|^p{d}y=\int_{\Omega^+}|u(x)|^p{d}x.$$
3. For $1\le p<\infty$,
   $$u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^+)\Longleftrightarrow v\in W^{1,p}(N^+),$$
   with equivalent norms, and
   $$v_{y_j}=\sum_i(u_{x_i}\circ\Phi^{-1})\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial y_j}.$$
4. A function in $W_0^{1,p}(\Omega^+)$ is carried to $W_0^{1,p}(N^+)$.

**Proof of (3)-(4)**
Choose smooth $u_k\to u$ in $W^{1,p}(\Omega^+)$. Put $v_k=u_k\circ\Phi^{-1}$. The change-of-variables formula gives $v_k\to v$ in $L^p$. By the classical chain rule and bounded $D\Phi^{-1}$,
$$\left\|(v_k)_{y_j}-\sum_i(u_{x_i}\circ\Phi^{-1})\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial y_j}\right\|_{L^p(N^+)}
\le C\|Du_k-Du\|_{L^p(\Omega^+)}\to0.$$
Pass to weak derivatives. Applying the same argument to $\Phi^{-1}$ gives the reverse estimate. For (4), approximate by functions compactly supported in $\Omega^+$; their pullbacks have compact support in $N^+$ and belong to $W_0^{1,p}(N^+)$.
**QED**

> [!remark]
> In the boundary estimate, a cutoff is used so that the localized function vanishes near the artificial edge of the chart. Zero trace on the original boundary alone is a condition on the flat boundary portion, not automatically on the whole boundary of the patch.

## Trace theorem

> [!thm] Trace
> Let $\Omega$ be a bdd $C^1$ domain and $1\le p<\infty$. There is a bounded linear operator
> $$T:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to L^p(\partial\Omega)$$
> such that $Tu=u|_{\partial\Omega}$ for $u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline\Omega)$ and
> $$\|Tu\|_{L^p(\partial\Omega)}\le C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}.$$

**Proof**
**Step 1. Estimate for $C^1(\overline\Omega)$ functions.** Choose a finite boundary atlas and a subordinate partition of unity $(\eta_i)$, and set $u_i=\eta_i u$. Flatten each chart and write $\widetilde u_i=u_i\circ\Phi_i^{-1}$. The boundary surface element is
$$\sqrt{\det G(y')}{d}y',\qquad G=(D_{y'}\Phi_i^{-1})^TD_{y'}\Phi_i^{-1},$$
whose density is bounded on each chart. After zero extension away from the chart,
$$\|Tu_i\|_{L^p(\partial\Omega)}^p\le C\int|\widetilde u_i(y',0)|^p{d}y'.$$
For $M$ beyond its support,
$$\widetilde u_i(y',0)=-\int_0^M\partial_{y_n}\widetilde u_i(y',s){d}s.$$
Hölder's inequality gives
$$\int|\widetilde u_i(y',0)|^p{d}y'\le M^{p-1}\int|\partial_{y_n}\widetilde u_i|^p{d}y
\le C\|u_i\|_{W^{1,p}}^p.$$
Sum over the finite partition, absorbing its size into $C$.

**Step 2. Definition by density.** For $u_k\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega)$ with $u_k\to u$ in $W^{1,p}$,
$$\|Tu_k-Tu_l\|_{L^p(\partial\Omega)}\le C\|u_k-u_l\|_{W^{1,p}}.$$
Thus $(Tu_k)$ is Cauchy. Define $Tu$ as its $L^p$ limit. Comparing two approximating sequences proves independence of the choice; passing to the limit proves boundedness and linearity. For continuous $u$, choose an approximation also converging uniformly on $\overline\Omega$, giving $Tu=u|_{\partial\Omega}$.
**QED**

### Refined trace estimate

For $1<p<\infty$, differentiate $|\widetilde u_i|^p$ in the normal direction:
$$|\widetilde u_i(y',0)|^p=-\int_0^M p|\widetilde u_i|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(\widetilde u_i)\partial_{y_n}\widetilde u_i\,{d}y_n.$$
Young's inequality and the charts give, for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$\|Tu\|_{L^p(\partial\Omega)}^p\le\varepsilon\|Du\|_{L^p(\Omega)}^p+C(\varepsilon,\Omega,p)\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}^p.$$

> [!note] 整理注
> The basic trace theorem includes $p=1$. The arbitrarily small coefficient $\varepsilon$ in this refined inequality requires $p>1$; the manuscript does not distinguish this endpoint.

> [!thm] Zero trace
> For a bdd $C^1$ domain,
> $$W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)=\ker T.$$

**Proof of $W_0^{1,p}\subset\ker T$**
Take $u_k\in C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ with $u_k\to u$ in $W^{1,p}$. Then $Tu_k=0$ and continuity of $T$ gives $Tu=0$.
**QED**

> [!remark]
> The manuscript states both directions but only proves this direction.

## Interpolation inequalities

> [!thm]
> Let $\Omega$ be a bdd $C^1$ domain, $u\in W^{m,p}(\Omega)$, $1\le p<\infty$. For $0<j<m$ and $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$,
> $$\sum_{|\alpha|=j}\|\partial^\alpha u\|_{L^p}
> \le K\left(\varepsilon\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\|\partial^\alpha u\|_{L^p}
> +\varepsilon^{-j/(m-j)}\|u\|_{L^p}\right),$$
> where $K=K(\varepsilon_0,m,p,\Omega)$.

> [!remark] Equivalent Sobolev norm
> $$\|u\|_{W^{m,p}}\le C\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\|\partial^\alpha u\|_{L^p}+\|u\|_{L^p}\right).$$

These estimates are used in [[5. Global H2-Regularity Theory]] and [[6. Lp-theory for strong solutions of elliptic equations]].
