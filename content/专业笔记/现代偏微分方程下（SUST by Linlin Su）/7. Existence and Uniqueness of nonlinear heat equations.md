# E & U of nonlinear heat eqs.

2026.6.1

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be bdd, $\partial\Omega\in C^{2+\alpha}$, $0<\alpha<1$, and $\kappa>0$. Consider
$$\begin{cases}u_t-\kappa\Delta u=f(x,t,u)&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\u(x,0)=\varphi(x)&\text{in }\Omega.
\end{cases}$$
Assume $f(\cdot,\cdot,u)$ is measurable for fixed $u$. On every bounded set $B\subset\overline\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}$ there is $M(B)$ s.t.
$$|f(x,t,u)|\le M(B),\qquad |f(x,t,u)-f(x,t,v)|\le M(B)|u-v|$$
whenever the indicated points lie in $B$.

## Maximal existence and blow-up alternative

> [!thm]
> For $\varphi\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, there exists a maximal $T_\varphi\in(0,\infty]$ and a unique solution with:
> 1. For all finite $p>1$ and $\varepsilon>0$ with $2\varepsilon<T_\varphi$,
> $$u\in W_p^{2,1}(\Omega\times(\varepsilon,T_\varphi-\varepsilon)),$$
> and the PDE holds a.e.; if $T_\varphi=\infty$, interpret this on every finite time strip away from $0$.
> 2. $u$ is bounded in $L^\infty$ on every compact time interval $[0,\tau]\subset[0,T_\varphi)$.
> 3. If $T_\varphi<\infty$, then
> $$\lim_{t\uparrow T_\varphi}\|u(t)\|_\infty=\infty.$$
> 4. The boundary condition holds classically for $t>0$, using parabolic Sobolev embedding with large $p$.
> 5. For every finite $p>1$,
> $$\lim_{t\downarrow0}\|u(t)-\varphi\|_{L^p(\Omega)}=0.$$

The manuscript states this E & U theorem without proof; the linear estimates are in [[9. Lp-theory for Parabolic Equations]].

If the local Lipschitz condition is strengthened to
$$|f(x,t,u)-f(y,s,v)|\le M\big(|x-y|^\alpha+|t-s|^{\alpha/2}+|u-v|\big),$$
then [[10. Schauder Theory for Parabolic Equations]] gives $u\in C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}$ on closed time strips away from $0$ and $T_\varphi$. If also $\varphi\in C^{2+\alpha}(\overline\Omega)$ and
$$\varphi|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad\kappa\Delta\varphi+f(x,0,\varphi)=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,$$
the same regularity extends to $t=0$ on every interval $[0,\tau]$, $\tau<T_\varphi$.

> [!remark]
> - Smooth $f$ and boundary give $u\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega\times(0,T_\varphi))$.
> - If $f(x,t,0)=0$ and $\varphi\ge0$, comparison gives $u\ge0$.
> - The manuscript records a Robin variant $\kappa\partial_\nu u+\beta(x)u=0$, $\beta\ge0$, $\beta\in C^{1+\alpha}$, with initial boundary compatibility $\kappa\partial_\nu\varphi+\beta\varphi=0$.

## Power nonlinearity

Consider
$$u_t-\Delta u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad u(0)=\varphi\ge0,\quad\varphi\not\equiv0,$$
where $1<p<\infty$. Then $u\ge0$, so the nonlinearity is $u^p$.

### Large data: Kaplan's eigenfunction method

Let $e_1>0$ be the first Dirichlet eigenfunction of $-\Delta$, $-\Delta e_1=\lambda_1e_1$, $\lambda_1>0$. Normalize
$$\int_\Omega e_1{d}x=1,\qquad {d}\mu=e_1{d}x.$$
Set $h(t)=\int_\Omega u(x,t)e_1(x){d}x$. Integration by parts and Jensen's inequality give
$$h'(t)+\lambda_1h(t)=\int_\Omega u^p{d}\mu\ge h(t)^p.$$
If
$$h(0)=\int_\Omega\varphi e_1{d}x>\lambda_1^{1/(p-1)},$$
then $h'>0$ and $h^p-\lambda_1h>0$. Consequently
$$t\le\int_{h(0)}^{h(t)}\frac{{d}s}{s^p-\lambda_1s}
\le\int_{h(0)}^\infty\frac{{d}s}{s^p-\lambda_1s}<\infty.$$
Thus $T_\varphi<\infty$.

> [!note] 整理注
> The normalization $\int e_1=1$ makes ${d}\mu$ a probability measure, as required by the displayed Jensen step. The bound $(s^p-\lambda_1s)^{-1}\le2s^{-p}$ is used only for sufficiently large $s$, not necessarily for every $s\ge h(0)$.

### Small data: comparison with a steady state

The manuscript cites as homework the existence of a positive steady state $\overline u$ satisfying
$$\Delta\overline u+\overline u^p=0,\qquad\overline u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,$$
for $1<p<(n+2)/(n-2)$ if $n\ge3$, and all finite $p>1$ for $n=1,2$.

Assume $0\le\varphi\le\theta\overline u$ for some $0<\theta<1$. Let $v$ solve the parabolic problem with initial datum $\theta\overline u$. Since
$$\Delta(\theta\overline u)+(\theta\overline u)^p=(\theta^p-\theta)\overline u^p<0,$$
$\theta\overline u$ is a stationary supersolution. Comparison gives
$$0\le u(t)\le v(t)\le\theta\overline u.$$
Hence both solutions are global, and $v(t)$ decreases in $t$. Parabolic regularity gives a stationary limit $v_\infty$ with $0\le v_\infty\le\theta\overline u$. Multiply the two stationary equations by the other solution and subtract:
$$0=\int_\Omega\overline u\,v_\infty\left(v_\infty^{p-1}-\overline u^{p-1}\right){d}x.$$
The integrand is negative wherever $v_\infty>0$, so $v_\infty=0$. Thus $u(x,t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$ (uniformly on $\overline\Omega$ using compactness).

## Fisher-KPP

The displayed PDE in the manuscript is
$$u_t=\Delta u+u\left(1-\frac uk\right),\qquad k>0,\qquad
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad u(0)=\varphi\ge0.$$
For a classical solution up to $t=0$, the notes impose $\varphi\in C^{2+\alpha}$, $\varphi|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ and
$$\Delta\varphi+\varphi(1-\varphi/k)=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.$$
The related ODE is the logistic equation $u'=ru(1-u/k)$, where $u(t)$ is total population and the PDE variable is population density.

Choose a constant $M\ge\max\{\|\varphi\|_\infty,k\}$. It is a supersolution, so
$$0\le u(x,t)\le M\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_\varphi=\infty.$$
For the displayed PDE, the large-time threshold is the principal eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ compared with the linear growth rate $1$:
$$
\begin{cases}
1>\lambda_1,\ \varphi\not\equiv0: &u(t)\to\text{the positive steady state},\\
1\le\lambda_1: &u(t)\to0.
\end{cases}
$$

> [!note] 整理注：the parameter in the threshold
> The final lines on p. 48 compare $k$ with $\lambda_1$, but in the displayed PDE $k$ is the carrying capacity. If the intended equation is $u_t=\Delta u+ru(1-u/k)$, the threshold is $r>\lambda_1$ versus $r\le\lambda_1$. For the equation actually displayed, $r=1$.
