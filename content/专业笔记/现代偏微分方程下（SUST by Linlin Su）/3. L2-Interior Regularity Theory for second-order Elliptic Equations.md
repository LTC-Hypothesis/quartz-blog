# $L^2$-Interior Regularity Theory for second-order Elliptic Eqs.

2025.3.11 (date as written in the manuscript)

Let $Lu=-(a_{ij}u_{x_i})_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}+cu$ be strictly elliptic on a bdd domain $\Omega$. A local weak sol $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ satisfies
$$
\int_\Omega(a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}v+cuv){d}x=\int_\Omega fv\,{d}x,\qquad v\in H_0^1(\Omega).
$$
The goal is $u\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ and, for $\Omega'\Subset\Omega$,
$$
\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega')}\le C\left(\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\right).
$$
The extra coefficient regularity needed for this conclusion is stated below.

## Difference quotients

> [!def]
> For $0<|h|<\operatorname{dist}(\Omega',\partial\Omega)$, define
> $$
> \nabla_i^hu(x)=\frac{u(x+he_i)-u(x)}h,\qquad
> \nabla^hu=(\nabla_1^hu,\ldots,\nabla_n^hu).
> $$

> [!thm] Difference-quotient estimates
> 1. If $1\le p<\infty$ and $u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$, then
> $$
> \|\nabla_i^hu\|_{L^p(\Omega')}\le\|u_{x_i}\|_{L^p(\Omega)}.
> $$
> 2. If $1<p<\infty$, $u\in L^p(\Omega)$ and $\|\nabla^hu\|_{L^p(\Omega')}\le C$ for all sufficiently small $h$, then $u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega')$ and $\|Du\|_{L^p(\Omega')}\le C$ (with compatible vector norms).

**Proof**
For smooth $u$,
$$
\nabla_i^hu(x)=\int_0^1u_{x_i}(x+the_i){d}t.
$$
Minkowski's inequality and translation give
$$
\|\nabla_i^hu\|_{L^p(\Omega')}\le\int_0^1\|u_{x_i}(\cdot+the_i)\|_{L^p(\Omega')}{d}t\le\|u_{x_i}\|_{L^p(\Omega)}.
$$
Approximate general $u\in W^{1,p}$ by smooth functions in $W^{1,p}$; for fixed $h$, translations and difference quotients converge in $L^p$.

For (2), reflexivity of $L^p$ gives a subsequence $\nabla^{h_k}u\rightharpoonup v$ as $h_k\to0$. For $\varphi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega')$, changing variables gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\Omega'}v_i\varphi\,{d}x
&=\lim_k\int_{\Omega'}\nabla_i^{h_k}u\,\varphi\,{d}x\\
&=-\lim_k\int_\Omega u\nabla_i^{-h_k}\varphi\,{d}x
=-\int_{\Omega'}u\varphi_{x_i}{d}x.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $v=Du$ in the weak sense. Weak lower semicontinuity gives the estimate.
**QED**

> [!remark] Integration by parts for difference quotients
> When the supports and shifts stay inside the domain,
> $$
> \int\nabla_i^hu\,\varphi\,{d}x=-\int u\nabla_i^{-h}\varphi\,{d}x.
> $$
> This remains valid for dual $L^p$ and $L^q$ functions. Near a flat boundary, the same argument works in the tangential directions $i=1,\ldots,n-1$.

## Interior $H^2$ estimate

2026.3.18

> [!thm] $L^2$-interior regularity
> Assume $a_{ij}\in C^1(\Omega)\cap L^\infty(\Omega)$, $b_i,c\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$ and $(a_{ij})\ge\lambda_0I$. If $f\in L^2(\Omega)$ and $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ is a weak sol of $Lu=f$, then
> $$
> u\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\qquad
> \|u\|_{H^2(\Omega')}\le C\left(\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\right)
> $$
> for every $\Omega'\Subset\Omega$. The constant depends on the nested domains, ellipticity, bounded lower-order coefficients and the local $C^1$ bounds of $a_{ij}$.

**Proof**
Choose $\Omega'\Subset\widetilde\Omega\Subset\Omega$.

**Step 1. Local $H^1$ estimate.** Choose $0\le\xi\le1$, $\xi\in C_0^\infty(\Omega)$, $\xi=1$ on $\widetilde\Omega$, and test with $v=\xi^2u$. Ellipticity and Young's inequality give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_\Omega[a_{ij}u_{x_i}(\xi^2u)_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}\xi^2u+c\xi^2u^2]{d}x
&\ge\frac{\lambda_0}{2}\int_\Omega\xi^2|Du|^2{d}x-C\int_\Omega u^2{d}x,\\
\left|\int_\Omega f\xi^2u\,{d}x\right|&\le\frac12\int_\Omega(f^2+u^2){d}x.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\|Du\|_{L^2(\widetilde\Omega)}^2\le C\left(\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}^2\right).\tag{1}
$$

**Step 2. Uniform difference-quotient estimate for $Du$.** Put $\widetilde f=f-b_i u_{x_i}-cu$. Take $\eta\in C_0^\infty(\widetilde\Omega)$ with $0\le\eta\le1$, $\eta=1$ on $\Omega'$, and test the principal-part equation with
$$
v=-\nabla_k^{-h}(\eta^2\nabla_k^hu).
$$
The discrete integration-by-parts formula gives
$$
\int\nabla_k^h(a_{ij}u_{x_i})(\eta^2\nabla_k^hu)_{x_j}{d}x
=\int\widetilde f\,v\,{d}x.
$$
Writing $T_k^ha_{ij}(x)=a_{ij}(x+he_k)$,
$$
\nabla_k^h(a_{ij}u_{x_i})=(T_k^ha_{ij})\nabla_k^hu_{x_i}+(\nabla_k^ha_{ij})u_{x_i}.
$$
The four terms on the left are
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\int\eta^2(T_k^ha_{ij})\nabla_k^hu_{x_i}\nabla_k^hu_{x_j},\\
I_2&=2\int\eta\eta_{x_j}(T_k^ha_{ij})\nabla_k^hu_{x_i}\nabla_k^hu,\\
I_3&=2\int\eta\eta_{x_j}(\nabla_k^ha_{ij})u_{x_i}\nabla_k^hu,\\
I_4&=\int\eta^2(\nabla_k^ha_{ij})u_{x_i}\nabla_k^hu_{x_j}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\nabla_k^ha_{ij}$ is uniformly bounded on the support,
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1&\ge\lambda_0\int\eta^2|\nabla_k^hDu|^2,\\
|I_2|&\le\frac{\lambda_0}{4}\int\eta^2|\nabla_k^hDu|^2+C\int|\nabla_k^hu|^2,\\
|I_3|&\le C\int(|Du|^2+|\nabla_k^hu|^2),\\
|I_4|&\le\frac{\lambda_0}{4}\int\eta^2|\nabla_k^hDu|^2+C\int|Du|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
By the first difference-quotient estimate,
$$
\|v\|_2\le\|\partial_{x_k}(\eta^2\nabla_k^hu)\|_2
\le C\|\nabla_k^hu\|_2+\|\eta\nabla_k^hDu\|_2.
$$
Young's inequality absorbs a further small multiple of $\|\eta\nabla_k^hDu\|_2^2$. Hence
$$
\int_{\Omega'}|\nabla_k^hDu|^2{d}x
\le C\left(\|f\|_{L^2(\widetilde\Omega)}^2+\|u\|_{H^1(\widetilde\Omega)}^2\right),\tag{2}$$
uniformly for small $h$. The converse difference-quotient theorem gives $D^2u\in L^2(\Omega')$. Combine (1) and (2).
**QED**

## Higher-order interior regularity

2026.3.23

If $a_{ij}\in C^2\cap L^\infty$, $b_i,c\in C^1\cap L^\infty$ and $f\in H^1$, then
$$
u\in H^3_{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad\|u\|_{H^3(\Omega')}\le C(\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{H^1(\Omega)}).
$$

**Proof**
First $u\in H^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Formally differentiate the equation and set $w=u_{x_k}$:
$$
Lw=\widetilde f:=f_{x_k}+\big((a_{ij})_{x_k}u_{x_i}\big)_{x_j}-(b_i)_{x_k}u_{x_i}-c_{x_k}u.
$$
On every smaller subdomain, $\widetilde f\in L^2$. To justify the identity weakly, use $v_{x_k}$ in the original weak equation for $v\in C_0^\infty$, integrate by parts, apply the weak product rule, and commute the second weak derivatives of $u$. By density, the identity holds for all $v\in H_0^1$ on that subdomain. The $H^2$ estimate applied to $w$ gives $u\in H^3_{\mathrm{loc}}$. On nested subdomains,
$$
\|u_{x_k}\|_{H^2(\Omega'')}\le C(\|\widetilde f\|_{L^2(\Omega')}+\|u_{x_k}\|_{L^2(\Omega')})\le C(\|f\|_{H^1(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}).
$$
**QED**

> [!thm] Higher-order I.R.
> If $a_{ij}\in C^{m+1}(\Omega)\cap L^\infty$, $b_i,c\in C^m(\Omega)\cap L^\infty$ and $f\in H^m(\Omega)$, then
> $$
> u\in H^{m+2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\qquad
> \|u\|_{H^{m+2}(\Omega')}\le C(\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{H^m(\Omega)}).
> $$
> For $m=0$, boundedness of $b_i,c$ suffices; continuity is unnecessary.

> [!corollary]
> If all coefficients and $f$ are $C^\infty$, then $u\in C^\infty(\Omega)$.

**Proof**
Iterate the previous argument locally, then use Sobolev embedding with an integer $s>k+n/2$ to obtain $u\in C^k$ on every smaller subdomain, for every $k$.
**QED**

Boundary estimates require the preparations in [[4. Global Regularity]] and are proved in [[5. Global H2-Regularity Theory]].
