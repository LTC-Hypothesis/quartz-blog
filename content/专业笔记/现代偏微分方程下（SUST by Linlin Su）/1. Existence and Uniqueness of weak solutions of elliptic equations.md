# Existence and Uniqueness of weak sol's of elliptic eqs.

2026.2.25 · [[L2-theory of elliptic equations]]

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a bdd domain. Consider
$$
Lu=-\sum_{i,j=1}^n(a_{ij}(x)u_{x_i})_{x_j}+\sum_{i=1}^nb_i(x)u_{x_i}+c(x)u.
$$
**Assumptions**: $a_{ij},b_i,c\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$ and
$$
\sum_{i,j=1}^na_{ij}(x)\xi_i\xi_j\ge\lambda_0|\xi|^2,\qquad \lambda_0>0.
$$
Repeated indices below are summed from $1$ to $n$.

> [!def] Weak solution
> Let $f\in(H_0^1(\Omega))^*=H^{-1}(\Omega)$. A weak sol of
> $$
> \begin{cases}Lu=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\end{cases}\tag{DBVP}
> $$
> is $u\in H_0^1(\Omega)$ satisfying
> $$
> \langle Lu,v\rangle:=\int_\Omega\left(a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}v+cuv\right){d}x
> =\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1},H_0^1},\quad\forall v\in H_0^1(\Omega).
> $$

> [!remark]
> $|\langle Lu,v\rangle|\le M\|u\|_{H_0^1}\|v\|_{H_0^1}$. Thus, for fixed $u$, $Lu$ is a bounded linear functional on $H_0^1(\Omega)$, and
> $$L:H_0^1(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega),\qquad \|Lu\|_{H^{-1}}\le M\|u\|_{H_0^1}$$
> is a bounded linear operator.

## Lax-Milgram

> [!thm] Lax-Milgram
> Let $H$ be a real Hilbert space and $a:H\times H\to\mathbb{R}$ a bilinear form. Assume $M,\gamma>0$ satisfy
> $$|a(u,v)|\le M\|u\|\|v\|,\qquad a(u,u)\ge\gamma\|u\|^2.$$
> Then $\forall f\in H^*$, $\exists!u\in H$ s.t.
> $$a(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle,\quad\forall v\in H,\qquad \|u\|_H\le\gamma^{-1}\|f\|_{H^*}.$$

> [!remark]
> If $a(u,v)=a(v,u)$, $a$ defines an equivalent inner product, so [[The Riesz Representation Theorem]] applies. Lax-Milgram also allows asymmetric bilinear forms.

> [!thm] E & U for a shifted operator
> There exists $\sigma_0>0$ depending on the coefficients and ellipticity s.t., for $\sigma\ge\sigma_0$ and $f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)$,
> $$\begin{cases}Lu+\sigma u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\end{cases}$$
> has a unique weak sol. With the full $H^1$ norm,
> $$\|u\|_{H_0^1}\le\frac{2}{\lambda_0}\|f\|_{H^{-1}}.$$

**Proof**
Define $L_\sigma=L+\sigma I$ and
$$
\mathcal L_\sigma(u,v)=\int_\Omega\left[a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}v+(c+\sigma)uv\right]{d}x.
$$
Boundedness follows from Hölder's inequality:
$$
|\mathcal L_\sigma(u,v)|\le C\|Du\|_2\|Dv\|_2+\|b\|_\infty\|Du\|_2\|v\|_2
+(\|c\|_\infty+\sigma)\|u\|_2\|v\|_2.
$$
For coercivity, Young's inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_\sigma(u,u)
&\ge\lambda_0\|Du\|_2^2-\|b\|_\infty\|Du\|_2\|u\|_2+(\sigma-\|c\|_\infty)\|u\|_2^2\\
&\ge\frac{\lambda_0}{2}\|Du\|_2^2+
\left(\sigma-\|c\|_\infty-\frac{\|b\|_\infty^2}{2\lambda_0}\right)\|u\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Choose $\sigma_0$ s.t. the last coefficient is at least $\lambda_0/2$. Apply Lax-Milgram.
**QED**

> [!remark]
> Without a sufficiently large shift, E & U is a harder question. We need Fredholm operator theory.

## Fredholm operators

For an $n\times n$ matrix $A$,
$$
\ker A=\{0\}\Longleftrightarrow R(A)=\mathbb{R}^n,
\qquad Ax=b\text{ is solvable}\Longleftrightarrow b\perp\ker A^T.
$$

> [!def] Fredholm operator and index
> Let $X,Y$ be Banach spaces and $T:X\to Y$ bounded and linear. $T$ is Fredholm if
> $$\dim\ker T<\infty,\qquad R(T)\text{ is closed},\qquad\operatorname{codim}R(T)<\infty.$$
> Its index is
> $$\operatorname{ind}T=\dim\ker T-\operatorname{codim}R(T).$$
> In particular, an $n\times n$ matrix has index $0$.

The adjoint $T^*:Y^*\to X^*$ is defined by
$$\langle T^*y^*,x\rangle=\langle y^*,Tx\rangle.$$
If $R(T)$ is closed, then
$$
R(T)=(\ker T^*)_\perp,
\qquad R(T^*)=(\ker T)^\perp.
$$
Here the first annihilator lies in $Y$ and the second in $X^*$. Moreover,
$$T\text{ Fredholm}\Longleftrightarrow T^*\text{ Fredholm},\qquad\operatorname{ind}T^*=-\operatorname{ind}T.$$

See also [[Compact Operators & Spectral Theory#The Riesz–Fredholm Theory]].

## Compact perturbation and DBVP

2025.3.4 (date as written in the manuscript)

> [!thm] Compact perturbation
> If $T:X\to Y$ is Fredholm and $K:X\to Y$ is compact, then $T+K$ is Fredholm and
> $$\operatorname{ind}(T+K)=\operatorname{ind}T.$$

> [!thm]
> $L:H_0^1(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)$ is Fredholm with $\operatorname{ind}L=0$.

**Proof**
The shifted operator $L_\sigma$ is an isomorphism, hence has index $0$. Write $L=L_\sigma-\sigma I$, where
$$
H_0^1(\Omega)\xrightarrow{I_1}L^2(\Omega)\xrightarrow{I_2}H^{-1}(\Omega),
\qquad\langle I_2u,v\rangle=\int_\Omega uv\,{d}x.
$$
$I_1$ is compact by the compact Sobolev embedding and $I_2$ is bounded. Thus $I=I_2I_1$ is compact. Apply the compact perturbation theorem.
**QED**

> [!corollary] Fredholm alternative for DBVP
> Exactly one of the following holds:
> 1. $Lu=0$, $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ has a nonzero weak sol.
> 2. For every $f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)$, DBVP has a unique weak sol.

> [!corollary] Compatibility condition
> DBVP is solvable iff
> $$\langle f,v\rangle_{H^{-1},H_0^1}=0,\quad\forall v\in\ker L^*.$$
> Also $\dim\ker L=\dim\ker L^*<\infty$, where
> $$L^*v=-(a_{ij}v_{x_j})_{x_i}-(b_iv)_{x_i}+cv$$
> in the distributional sense and $\langle Lu,v\rangle=\langle L^*v,u\rangle$.

Under the symmetry assumption on $a_{ij}$, $L=L^*$ iff $b_i=0$ for all $i$.

### Double dual and reflexivity

For $X=H_0^1(\Omega)$, $L:X\to X^*$ has Banach adjoint $L_F^*:X^{**}\to X^*$. The canonical map
$$j:X\to X^{**},\qquad\langle j(x),x^*\rangle=\langle x^*,x\rangle$$
is onto because $X$ is reflexive. Thus the formal adjoint above is identified with $L_F^*j:X\to X^*$.

> [!example]
> Let $\Omega=(0,\pi)$ and $Lu=-u''-u$. The homogeneous Dirichlet problem has
> $$u(x)=C_1\cos x+C_2\sin x,\qquad u(0)=u(\pi)=0,$$
> so $\ker L=\operatorname{span}\{\sin x\}$. For $f\in L^2(0,\pi)$, the compatibility condition becomes
> $$\int_0^\pi f(x)\sin x\,{d}x=0.$$

The self-adjoint case leads to [[2. Eigenvalue Problems]].
