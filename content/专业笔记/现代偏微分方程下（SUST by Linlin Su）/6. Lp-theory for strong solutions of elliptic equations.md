# $L^p$-theory for strong sol's of elliptic eqs. ($1<p<\infty$)

2026.4.20

In this section the sign convention is
$$
Lu=a_{ij}(x)u_{x_ix_j}+b_i(x)u_{x_i}+c(x)u,
$$
where $a_{ij},b_i,c\in L^\infty$ and $(a_{ij})$ is uniformly positive definite.

> [!def] Strong solution
> A strong sol of $Lu=f\in L^p(\Omega)$, $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$, is
> $$
> u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_0^{1,p}(\Omega)
> $$
> satisfying the equation a.e. in $\Omega$.

## Fredholm alternative and $L^p$ estimates

> [!thm] Fredholm alternative
> If $\partial\Omega\in C^2$ and $a_{ij}\in C(\overline\Omega)$, uniqueness for the homogeneous Dirichlet problem is equivalent to existence and uniqueness of a strong sol for every $f\in L^p$.

> [!remark]
> With the present positive-principal-part convention, $c\le0$ implies uniqueness by the maximum principle.

> [!thm] Interior estimate
> If $a_{ij}\in C(\overline\Omega)$ and $u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)$ solves $Lu=f$, then
> $$
> \|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega')}\le C(\|f\|_{L^p(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}),\qquad\Omega'\Subset\Omega.
> $$

> [!thm] Global estimate
> If also $\partial\Omega\in C^2$ and $u\in W^{2,p}\cap W_0^{1,p}$, then
> $$
> \|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\le C(\|f\|_{L^p(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}).
> $$

Constants depend on $n,p,\lambda_0$, the coefficient bounds and continuity of $a_{ij}$, and the domains.

## $L^p$ regularity and bootstrap

> [!thm]
> Let $1<p<q<\infty$ and $a_{ij}\in C(\overline\Omega)$.
> 1. If $\partial\Omega\in C^2$, $u\in W^{2,p}\cap W_0^{1,p}$ and $Lu=f\in L^q$, then $u\in W^{2,q}\cap W_0^{1,q}$.
> 2. In the interior, $u\in W^{2,p}$ and $f\in L^q$ imply $u\in W^{2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

**Proof of the global statement (bootstrap)**
Sobolev embedding gives $u\in L^{p_1}$, where
$$
p_1=\frac{np}{n-2p}\quad(2p<n),
$$
and $p_1$ can be chosen arbitrarily large and finite if $2p\ge n$. Solve
$$
\begin{cases}a_{ij}v_{x_ix_j}+b_iv_{x_i}=f-cu&\text{in }\Omega,\\v|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}\tag{1}
$$
The zero-order coefficient of this auxiliary operator is zero, so uniqueness and the Fredholm theorem apply.

If $p_1\ge q$, then the right-hand side is in $L^q$ and there is $v\in W^{2,q}\cap W_0^{1,q}$. Both $u$ and $v$ solve (1) in the $L^p$ setting, so $u=v$. If $p_1<q$, first obtain $u\in W^{2,p_1}$ and repeat. Before the critical exponent,
$$
\frac1{p_{k+1}}=\frac1{p_k}-\frac2n.
$$
After finitely many steps one reaches the target exponent or the range with arbitrarily large finite integrability.
**QED**

> [!thm] Higher-order $L^p$ regularity
> Let $m\ge1$, $1<p\le q<\infty$, and $f\in W^{m,q}$.
> - Global: if $\partial\Omega\in C^{m+2}$ and $a_{ij},b_i,c\in C^m(\overline\Omega)$, then a strong Dirichlet sol $u\in W^{2,p}\cap W_0^{1,p}$ lies in $W^{m+2,q}\cap W_0^{1,q}$.
> - Interior: with local $C^m$ coefficients, $u\in W^{m+2,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}$.
> Smooth data and coefficients give smooth solutions in the corresponding region.

## Schauder theory

Assume $a_{ij},b_i,c\in C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)$, $0<\alpha<1$, uniform ellipticity, and $\partial\Omega\in C^{2+\alpha}$ for boundary statements.

> [!thm] Fredholm alternative in Hölder spaces
> Uniqueness for $Lu=0$, $u|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ is equivalent to existence and uniqueness of a $C^{2+\alpha}(\overline\Omega)$ sol for every $f\in C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)$.

2026.4.22

> [!remark]
> If $c\le0$, the weak maximum principle applied to $u$ and $-u$ gives uniqueness.
> Under weaker boundary assumptions, if $\Omega$ satisfies the exterior sphere condition, $a_{ij},b_i,c\in C^\alpha_{\mathrm{loc}}\cap L^\infty$, $c\le0$, $f\in C^\alpha_{\mathrm{loc}}\cap L^\infty$ and $\varphi\in C(\partial\Omega)$, there is a unique
> $$
> u\in C(\overline\Omega)\cap C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\qquad Lu=f,\quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=\varphi.
> $$

> [!thm] Schauder estimates
> Interior:
> $$
> \|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha}(\overline{\Omega'})}\le C(\|f\|_{C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)}+\|u\|_{C(\overline\Omega)}),\quad\Omega'\Subset\Omega.
> $$
> Global, for zero Dirichlet data:
> $$
> \|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha}(\overline\Omega)}\le C(\|f\|_{C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)}+\|u\|_{C(\overline\Omega)}).
> $$

### Strong solutions become classical

> [!thm] User-friendly regularity theorem
> If $u\in W^{2,p}\cap W_0^{1,p}$ is a strong sol, $a_{ij},b_i,c,f\in C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)$ and $\partial\Omega\in C^{2+\alpha}$, then $u\in C^{2+\alpha}(\overline\Omega)$.
> The corresponding interior statement is $u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\Longrightarrow u\in C^{2+\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for locally Hölder coefficients and data.

**Proof**
Globally, $f\in L^q$ for every finite $q$, so bootstrap gives $u\in W^{2,q}$ for every $q$. Choose $q$ large enough to have $u\in C^\alpha$. Then $f-cu\in C^\alpha$. Solve (1) by Schauder theory; uniqueness in $W^{2,p}$ identifies its classical sol with $u$.

Locally, bootstrap first gives $u\in C^{1+\alpha}$ on smaller subdomains. Choose $\Omega'\Subset\Omega_1\Subset\Omega$, with $\Omega_1$ smooth, and $\eta\in C_0^\infty(\Omega_1)$ equal to $1$ on $\Omega'$. Then
$$
L(\eta u)=\eta f+a_{ij}\eta_{x_ix_j}u+a_{ij}(\eta_{x_i}u_{x_j}+\eta_{x_j}u_{x_i})+b_i\eta_{x_i}u.
$$
The right-hand side is $C^\alpha$, and $\eta u=0$ on $\partial\Omega_1$. Apply the global statement on $\Omega_1$.
**QED**

## Weak solutions become strong

Return to the divergence-form convention
$$
Au=-(a_{ij}u_{x_i})_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}+cu.
$$

> [!thm]
> Suppose $\partial\Omega\in C^2$, $a_{ij}\in C^1(\overline\Omega)$, $b_i,c\in L^\infty$, and $u\in H_0^1$ is a weak sol of $Au=f\in L^p$, where
> $$
> p\ge\frac{2n}{n+2}\quad(n\ge3),\qquad p>1\quad(n=1,2),\qquad p<\infty.
> $$
> Then $u\in W^{2,p}\cap W_0^{1,p}$ and it is a strong sol of
> $$
> -a_{ij}u_{x_ix_j}+(b_i-\partial_{x_j}a_{ij})u_{x_i}+cu=f.
> $$

The restriction on $p$ ensures $f\in H^{-1}$, because
$$
\left|\int_\Omega fv\,{d}x\right|\le\|f\|_p\|v\|_{p'}\le C\|f\|_p\|v\|_{H_0^1},
$$
using $H_0^1\hookrightarrow L^{2n/(n-2)}$ for $n\ge3$ and all finite exponents for $n=1,2$.

**Proof**
If $p\ge2$, [[5. Global H2-Regularity Theory]] first gives $u\in H^2$. Integration by parts identifies the nondivergence equation a.e.; $L^p$ regularity then gives $W^{2,p}$.

If $1<p<2$, let
$$
\widetilde Lv=-a_{ij}v_{x_ix_j}+(b_i-\partial_{x_j}a_{ij})v_{x_i}
$$
and solve $\widetilde Lv=f-cu$, $v|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ in $W^{2,p}\cap W_0^{1,p}$. Here $u\in L^2\subset L^p$. Sobolev embedding and $p\ge2n/(n+2)$ give $v,Dv\in L^2$, so $v\in H^1$.

To identify its trace in $H^1$, approximate $v$ by $v_k\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega)$ in $H^1$. Then also $v_k\to v$ in $W^{1,p}$. The $H^1$ and $W^{1,p}$ trace limits agree in $L^p(\partial\Omega)$, hence $T^{(2)}v=T^{(p)}v=0$ and $v\in H_0^1$.

Both $u$ and $v$ solve the same weak problem
$$
-(a_{ij}w_{x_i})_{x_j}+b_iw_{x_i}=f-cu,\qquad w|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
$$
The zero-order coefficient is zero. Uniqueness from the weak maximum principle (cited in the manuscript as HW1, T6) yields $u=v$.
**QED**

> [!note] 整理注
> At $p=2n/(n+2)$, the embedding for $Dv$ gives $L^2$, not necessarily an exponent strictly greater than $2$. This is sufficient. Also, the auxiliary weak equation must keep $f-cu$ on the right; the original equation with a $cv$ term is recovered only after $u=v$.

> [!corollary] Weak solutions become classical
> If additionally $a_{ij}\in C^{1+\alpha}(\overline\Omega)$, $b_i,c,f\in C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)$ and $\partial\Omega\in C^{2+\alpha}$, then $u\in C^{2+\alpha}(\overline\Omega)$.

**Proof**
First obtain $W^{2,p}$ for all finite $p$, then apply the strong-to-classical result.
**QED**

> [!remark] Robin / Neumann condition
> The manuscript also records corresponding weak-to-strong and weak-to-classical results for $\partial_{\nu_A}u+\beta u=0$: $\beta\in C^1(\partial\Omega)$ in the former, and $\beta\in C^{1+\alpha}(\partial\Omega)$ in the latter, with the corresponding coefficient and boundary assumptions. No proof is supplied there.
