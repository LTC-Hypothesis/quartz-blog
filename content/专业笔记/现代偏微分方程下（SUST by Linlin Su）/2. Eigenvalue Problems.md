# Eigenvalue Problems

[[L2-theory of elliptic equations]] · [[1. Existence and Uniqueness of weak solutions of elliptic equations]]

Consider the self-adjoint problem
$$
\begin{cases}
Lu=-(a_{ij}u_{x_i})_{x_j}+cu=\lambda u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
\end{cases}\tag{EP}
$$
Assume $a_{ij},c\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$ and $(a_{ij})\ge\lambda_0I$, $\lambda_0>0$.
Write
$$\mathcal L(u,v)=\int_\Omega(a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}+cuv){d}x.$$

> [!def] Eigenvalue and Rayleigh quotient
> $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue if $\exists u\in H_0^1(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}$ s.t.
> $$\mathcal L(u,v)=\lambda(u,v)_{L^2},\qquad\forall v\in H_0^1(\Omega).$$
> Set
> $$R(u)=\frac{\mathcal L(u,u)}{\|u\|_2^2},\qquad\lambda_1=\inf_{0\ne u\in H_0^1(\Omega)}R(u).$$

## The first eigenvalue

> [!lemma] Attainment of the Rayleigh quotient
> $\lambda_1$ is an eigenvalue of (EP).

**Proof**
Ellipticity gives $R(u)\ge-\|c\|_\infty$, hence $\lambda_1\in\mathbb{R}$. Choose a minimizing sequence, normalized as
$$\|v_k\|_2=1,\qquad R(v_k)=\mathcal L(v_k,v_k)\to\lambda_1.$$
Since
$$\lambda_0\|Dv_k\|_2^2\le R(v_k)+\|c\|_\infty,$$
$(v_k)$ is bounded in $H_0^1$. After passing to a subsequence,
$$v_k\rightharpoonup v_\infty\text{ in }H_0^1,\qquad v_k\to v_\infty\text{ in }L^2,\qquad\|v_\infty\|_2=1.$$
The inner product
$$((u,v))=\int_\Omega a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}{d}x$$
induces an equivalent norm on $H_0^1$, by ellipticity and Poincaré's inequality. Weak lower semicontinuity and strong $L^2$ convergence imply
$$
\lambda_1=\liminf_k\mathcal L(v_k,v_k)
\ge ((v_\infty,v_\infty))+\int_\Omega cv_\infty^2{d}x
=R(v_\infty)\ge\lambda_1.
$$
For any $\omega\in H_0^1$, $t=0$ minimizes $R(v_\infty+t\omega)$. If
$$h(t)=\mathcal L(v_\infty+t\omega,v_\infty+t\omega),\qquad g(t)=\|v_\infty+t\omega\|_2^2,$$
then $h(0)=\lambda_1$, $g(0)=1$, $h'(0)=2\mathcal L(v_\infty,\omega)$ and $g'(0)=2(v_\infty,\omega)_2$. Thus
$$0=\left.\frac{{d}}{{d}t}\frac{h(t)}{g(t)}\right|_{t=0}
\Longrightarrow\mathcal L(v_\infty,\omega)=\lambda_1(v_\infty,\omega)_2.$$
**QED**

> [!note] 整理注
> The manuscript calls the lower-semicontinuity step “Fatou lemma”. Here the relevant fact is weak lower semicontinuity of the Hilbert norm; weak convergence alone does not give pointwise convergence of gradients.

For a symmetric matrix $A$, the analogous construction is
$$\lambda_1=\inf_{x\ne0}\frac{x^TAx}{|x|^2},\qquad
\lambda_k=\inf_{x\ne0,\ x\perp y_1,\ldots,y_{k-1}}\frac{x^TAx}{|x|^2},$$
where $(y_1,\ldots,y_n)$ is an orthonormal eigenbasis.

> [!remark]
> All eigenvalues are real: test a complex eigenfunction with $\overline u$ to obtain
> $$\int_\Omega(a_{ij}u_{x_i}\overline{u_{x_j}}+c|u|^2){d}x=\lambda\|u\|_2^2,$$
> whose left-hand side is real. Every eigenpair satisfies $\lambda=R(u)\ge\lambda_1$.

## Sign and simplicity

> [!lemma]
> If $\Omega$ is connected, every real eigenfunction for $\lambda_1$ has one strict sign in $\Omega$, and $\dim E_{\lambda_1}=1$.

**Proof**
If $u_1\in E_{\lambda_1}\setminus\{0\}$, then $|u_1|\in H_0^1$ and $R(|u_1|)=R(u_1)=\lambda_1$. Thus $|u_1|$ is also an eigenfunction.

Recall the De Giorgi-Nash regularity theorem: weak solutions of a strictly elliptic equation with bounded coefficients are locally Hölder continuous under the assumptions used here. The Harnack inequality for a nonnegative solution gives, on compactly contained connected subdomains,
$$\sup_{\Omega'}u\le C\inf_{\Omega'}u.$$
Apply it locally to $|u_1|$ and propagate along overlapping balls. A zero would force $|u_1|\equiv0$, so $u_1$ cannot change sign.

If $u_1,u_2$ were linearly independent in $E_{\lambda_1}$, then
$$u_3=u_1-\frac{(u_1,u_2)_2}{\|u_2\|_2^2}u_2\ne0,\qquad u_3\perp u_2.$$
Both $u_2$ and $u_3$ have one strict sign, contradicting $\int_\Omega u_2u_3{d}x=0$.
**QED**

> [!note] 整理注
> Connectedness is used in the sign and simplicity argument. It is included in the usual meaning of “domain”; the conclusion need not hold for a disconnected open set.

## Higher eigenvalues

Take $\|u_1\|_2=1$. Define
$$F_1=\{u\in H_0^1:(u,u_1)_2=0\},\qquad\lambda_2=\inf_{0\ne u\in F_1}R(u).$$

**Proof of attainment**
A normalized minimizing sequence converges weakly in $H_0^1$ and strongly in $L^2$ to a minimizer $v_\infty\in F_1$. Variations within $F_1$ give
$$\mathcal L(v_\infty,\omega)=\lambda_2(v_\infty,\omega)_2,\quad\omega\in F_1.$$
For arbitrary $v=\alpha u_1+\omega$, symmetry yields
$$\mathcal L(v_\infty,u_1)=\mathcal L(u_1,v_\infty)=\lambda_1(u_1,v_\infty)_2=0.$$
Thus the eigenvalue identity holds for every $v\in H_0^1$.
**QED**

Simplicity of $\lambda_1$ implies $\lambda_2>\lambda_1$. Inductively,
$$
\lambda_{k+1}=\inf_{\substack{0\ne u\in H_0^1\\u\perp u_1,\ldots,u_k}}R(u),
\qquad\|u_{k+1}\|_2=1,\quad u_{k+1}\perp u_1,\ldots,u_k.
$$

> [!lemma]
> $\lambda_k\to+\infty$ as $k\to\infty$.

**Proof**
If $(\lambda_k)$ were bounded, then
$$\lambda_0\|Du_k\|_2^2\le\lambda_k+\|c\|_\infty$$
would make $(u_k)$ bounded in $H_0^1$. Compact embedding would give an $L^2$ convergent subsequence, contradicting
$$\|u_k-u_m\|_2^2=2\quad(k\ne m).$$
**QED**

## Completeness

Equip $H_0^1$ with the equivalent inner product
$$((u,v))_* =\mathcal L(u,v)+\|c\|_\infty(u,v)_2.$$
Set $C_k=(\lambda_k+\|c\|_\infty)^{-1/2}$.

> [!lemma]
> $(C_ku_k)_{k\ge1}$ is an orthonormal basis of $(H_0^1,((\cdot,\cdot))_*)$.

**Proof**
$$((C_ku_k,C_mu_m))_*=C_kC_m(\lambda_k+\|c\|_\infty)\delta_{km}=\delta_{km}.$$
If $u$ is orthogonal to every $u_k$ in this inner product, then
$$0=((u,u_k))_*=(\lambda_k+\|c\|_\infty)(u,u_k)_2,$$
so $u\perp_{L^2}u_k$ for every $k$. If $u\ne0$, the variational definition gives $\lambda_{k+1}\le R(u)$ for all $k$, contradicting $\lambda_k\to\infty$.
**QED**

> [!lemma]
> $(u_k)_{k\ge1}$ is an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\Omega)$.

**Proof**
For $u\in H_0^1$, the previous expansion reduces to
$$u=\sum_{k=1}^\infty((u,C_ku_k))_*C_ku_k
=\sum_{k=1}^\infty(u,u_k)_2u_k$$
in $H_0^1$, hence in $L^2$. For general $u\in L^2$, choose $\varphi_m\in C_0^\infty(\Omega)$ with $\varphi_m\to u$ in $L^2$. If $P_Nu=\sum_{k\le N}(u,u_k)_2u_k$, the best-approximation property gives
$$\|u-P_Nu\|_2\le\|u-P_N\varphi_m\|_2.$$
Let $N\to\infty$, then $m\to\infty$.
**QED**

> [!lemma] Exhaustion of the spectrum
> Every eigenvalue is one of the $\lambda_k$, and
> $$E_\lambda=\operatorname{span}\{u_k:\lambda_k=\lambda\}$$
> is finite-dimensional.

**Proof**
For an eigenpair $(\lambda,e)$, symmetry implies
$$\lambda(e,u_k)_2=\mathcal L(e,u_k)=\lambda_k(e,u_k)_2.$$
Thus $(e,u_k)_2=0$ unless $\lambda=\lambda_k$. Completeness gives the stated expansion, and $\lambda_k\to\infty$ gives finite multiplicity.
**QED**
