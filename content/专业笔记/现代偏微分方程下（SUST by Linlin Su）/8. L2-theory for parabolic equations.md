# $L^2$-theory for parabolic eqs.

2026.4.29

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be bdd, $0<T<\infty$, $Q_T=\Omega\times(0,T)$ and $S_T=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$. Consider
$$
\begin{cases}u_t+Lu=f(x,t)&\text{in }Q_T,\\
u=0&\text{on }S_T,\\
u(x,0)=\varphi(x)&\text{in }\Omega,
\end{cases}\tag{IBVP}
$$
where
$$
Lu=-(a_{ij}(x,t)u_{x_i})_{x_j}+b_i(x,t)u_{x_i}+c(x,t)u.
$$

## Anisotropic Sobolev spaces

> [!def]
> For $k\ge1$ and $1\le p<\infty$,
> $$
> W_p^{2k,k}(Q_T)=\{u\in L^p(Q_T):\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^\beta u\in L^p,\ |\alpha|+2\beta\le2k\},
> $$
> with norm
> $$
> \|u\|_{W_p^{2k,k}}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|+2\beta\le2k}\|\partial_x^\alpha\partial_t^\beta u\|_p^p\right)^{1/p}.
> $$
> In particular,
> $$
> W_p^{2,1}(Q_T)=\{u:u,u_t,D_xu,D_x^2u\in L^p(Q_T)\}.
> $$

These are Banach spaces; for $p=2$ they are Hilbert spaces. The manuscript also uses
$$
W_p^{0,0}=L^p,\qquad W_p^{0,1}=\{u,u_t\in L^p\},\qquad W_p^{1,0}=\{u,D_xu\in L^p\}.
$$
It identifies $W_p^{1,1}(Q_T)$ with ordinary $W^{1,p}(Q_T)$, so only first derivatives $u_{x_i}$ and $u_t$ are intended, without mixed second derivatives. We use that meaning throughout this note.

> [!def] Lateral zero-boundary spaces
> $\dot W_p^{\ell,k}$ and $\dot W_p^{2k,k}$ denote the closures, in the indicated norms, of smooth functions on $\overline{Q_T}$ that vanish near $\overline{S_T}$. This condition concerns the lateral boundary; it does not impose zero initial data.

> [!remark]
> At high spatial order, closure of functions vanishing near the lateral boundary can impose additional derivative traces. It should not be identified with the condition $u|_{S_T}=0$ alone.

The manuscript records smooth density in these Sobolev spaces for regular domains. For high order, the extension argument below is used with sufficient boundary regularity.

## Time-continuity embeddings

> [!thm] Embedding 1
> For $1<p<\infty$,
> $$
> W_p^{0,1}(Q_T)\hookrightarrow C([0,T];L^p(\Omega)),\qquad
> \max_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)\|_p\le\left(p+\frac1T\right)^{1/p}\|u\|_{W_p^{0,1}}.
> $$

**Proof**
For smooth $u$,
$$
\frac{{d}}{{d}t}\int_\Omega|u|^p{d}x=\int_\Omega p|u|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(u)u_t{d}x.
$$
Integrating between $s$ and $t$, Hölder's inequality bounds the difference of the $p$th powers by $p\|u\|_{W_p^{0,1}}^p$. Choose $s$ with
$$
\|u(s)\|_p^p=\frac1T\int_0^T\|u(t)\|_p^p{d}t.
$$
This gives the displayed estimate. Approximate general $u$ by smooth $u_k$ in $W_p^{0,1}$. The estimate makes $(u_k)$ Cauchy in $C([0,T];L^p)$; its limit agrees a.e. with $u$.
**QED**

2026.5.6

> [!thm] Embedding 2
> On a sufficiently smooth bounded domain,
> $$
> W_2^{2k,k}(Q_T)\hookrightarrow C([0,T];H^{2k-1}(\Omega)).
> $$
> More generally, for $0\le j\le k-1$,
> $$
> \partial_t^ju\in C([0,T];H^{2k-2j-1}(\Omega)),\qquad
> \|\partial_t^ju\|_{C_tH_x^{2k-2j-1}}\le C\|u\|_{W_2^{2k,k}}.
> $$

**Proof**
The manuscript uses a bounded extension $E:W_p^{2k,k}(Q_T)\to W_p^{2k,k}(\Omega'\times(0,T))$, $\Omega\Subset\Omega'$, with spatially compact support after a cutoff. Such an extension is not unique. For $k=1$, smooth approximation and spatial integration by parts give
$$
\frac{{d}}{{d}t}\int_{\Omega'}|Du|^2{d}x=-2\int_{\Omega'}\Delta u\,u_t{d}x.
$$
Therefore
$$
\left|\|Du(t)\|_2^2-\|Du(s)\|_2^2\right|\le\|\Delta u\|_{L^2(Q'_T)}^2+\|u_t\|_{L^2(Q'_T)}^2.
$$
Choose an average time $s$ and use Embedding 1 to control $u$ itself. This yields the $C_tH_x^1$ estimate; density gives continuity. Apply the same argument to spatial derivatives of order at most $2k-2$, and then to $\partial_t^ju$.
**QED**

> [!note] 整理注
> The theorem line on p. 34 writes $\partial\Omega\in C^2$ for all $k$, while the extension argument there uses $C^{2k}$. Taking $\partial\Omega\in C^{2k}$ is sufficient for the proof as written. In the lateral closure spaces, the corresponding time traces lie in $H_0^{2k-1}$.

## Weak solutions and energy estimate

Assume $a_{ij},b_i,c\in L^\infty(Q_T)$ and uniform parabolicity
$$
a_{ij}(x,t)\xi_i\xi_j\ge\lambda_0|\xi|^2.
$$

> [!def] Weak sol (the manuscript's class)
> A function $u\in\dot W_2^{1,1}(Q_T)$ is a weak sol if $u(t)\to\varphi$ in $L^2$ as $t\downarrow0$ and
> $$
> \iint_{Q_T}(u_tv+a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}+b_iu_{x_i}v+cuv){d}x{d}t=\iint_{Q_T}fv\,{d}x{d}t
> $$
> for admissible tests $v\in\dot W_2^{1,1}(Q_T)$.

Multiplying $v$ by smooth time cutoffs converging to $\mathbf1_{[0,\tau)}$ gives the same identity on $Q_\tau$ for every $\tau\in[0,T]$.

> [!note] 整理注：initial-data regularity
> The definition above requires $u_t\in L^2(Q_T)$. For arbitrary $\varphi\in L^2(\Omega)$, the usual energy-solution space is instead
> $$
> u\in L^2(0,T;H_0^1),\qquad u_t\in L^2(0,T;H^{-1}),\qquad u\in C([0,T];L^2).
> $$
> The explicit existence proof below assumes $\varphi\in H_0^1$ and a time-independent self-adjoint operator. It does not establish the stronger $\dot W_2^{1,1}$ conclusion for arbitrary $L^2$ initial data or merely measurable time-dependent coefficients.

> [!thm] Energy estimate
> A weak sol in the manuscript's class satisfies
> $$
> \max_{0\le t\le T}\|u(t)\|_2^2+\iint_{Q_T}|Du|^2{d}x{d}t
> \le C\left(\|\varphi\|_2^2+\|f\|_{L^2(Q_T)}^2\right).
> $$

**Proof**
Test on $Q_\tau$ with $v=u$. Smooth approximation and Embedding 1 justify
$$
\iint_{Q_\tau}u_tu\,{d}x{d}t=\frac12\left(\|u(\tau)\|_2^2-\|\varphi\|_2^2\right).
$$
Ellipticity and Young's inequality yield
$$
\|u(\tau)\|_2^2+\lambda_0\int_0^\tau\|Du(t)\|_2^2{d}t
\le A+C\int_0^\tau\|u(t)\|_2^2{d}t,
$$
where $A=\|\varphi\|_2^2+C\|f\|_{L^2(Q_T)}^2$. Set $g(t)=\int_0^t\|u(s)\|_2^2{d}s$. Then $g'(t)\le A+Cg(t)$ and $g(0)=0$. Multiplying by $e^{-Ct}$ and integrating gives $g(t)\le A(e^{Ct}-1)/C$. Substitute this into the preceding estimate.
**QED**

2026.5.13, 2026.5.18, 2026.5.20

> [!thm] Uniqueness
> IBVP has at most one weak sol in this class.

**Proof**
The difference of two solutions has zero forcing and initial data. The energy estimate forces it to be zero.
**QED**

## Galerkin approximation

The manuscript proves existence for
$$
Lu=-(a_{ij}(x)u_{x_i})_{x_j}+c(x)u,
$$
with real symmetric, bounded, uniformly elliptic $a_{ij}$ and bounded $c$, all independent of $t$.

By [[2. Eigenvalue Problems]], choose an $L^2$ orthonormal eigenbasis $(e_k)$ with $Le_k=\lambda_ke_k$, $\lambda_k\to\infty$. The eigenfunctions are orthogonal in the shifted energy inner product, and their expansions converge in $H_0^1$ for $H_0^1$ data.

Write formally
$$
u(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty C_k(t)e_k(x),\qquad f(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty d_k(t)e_k(x),\quad d_k(t)=(f(t),e_k)_2.
$$
The coefficient equations are
$$
C_k'+\lambda_kC_k=d_k,\qquad C_k(0)=(\varphi,e_k)_2,
$$
so
$$
C_k(t)=e^{-\lambda_kt}(\varphi,e_k)_2+\int_0^te^{-\lambda_k(t-s)}d_k(s){d}s.\tag{1}
$$

> [!thm] Special existence theorem
> If $f\in L^2(Q_T)$ and $\varphi\in H_0^1(\Omega)$, the series (1) defines the unique weak sol, with
> $$
> u\in\dot W_2^{1,1}(Q_T)\cap C([0,T];H_0^1(\Omega)),
> $$
> and
> $$
> \max_t\|u(t)\|_{H_0^1}+\|u\|_{W_2^{1,1}(Q_T)}\le C(\|f\|_{L^2(Q_T)}+\|\varphi\|_{H_0^1}).
> $$

**Proof**
**Step 1. Project the data.** Let
$$
f_m=\sum_{k=1}^md_ke_k,\qquad\varphi_m=\sum_{k=1}^m(\varphi,e_k)_2e_k.
$$
Fubini gives $f(t)\in L^2$ for a.e. $t$. Orthogonal projections and dominated convergence show
$$
f_m\to f\text{ in }L^2(Q_T),\qquad\varphi_m\to\varphi\text{ in }H_0^1.
$$
For domination one can use $\|f_m(t)-f(t)\|_2^2\le\|f(t)\|_2^2$.

**Step 2. Finite-dimensional solutions.** Since $d_k\in L^2(0,T)$, (1) gives $C_k\in H^1(0,T)$ and $C_k'=d_k-\lambda_kC_k$. Define
$$
u_m=\sum_{k=1}^mC_ke_k.
$$
For a.e. $t$,
$$
Lu_m=f_m-(u_m)_t\quad\text{in }H^{-1},\qquad u_m(0)=\varphi_m.
$$
Testing and integrating in time gives the weak equation for $u_m$.

**Step 3. Estimate the time derivative.** The basic energy estimate controls $u_m$ and $Du_m$. Test the elliptic identity with $(u_m)_t$. With
$$
\mathcal L(v,v)=\int_\Omega(a_{ij}v_{x_i}v_{x_j}+cv^2){d}x,
$$
time independence and symmetry give
$$
\frac12\frac{{d}}{{d}t}\mathcal L(u_m,u_m)+\|(u_m)_t\|_2^2=(f_m,(u_m)_t)_2.
$$
Integrate and apply Young's inequality:
$$
\lambda_0\|Du_m(t)\|_2^2+\int_0^t\|(u_m)_t\|_2^2{d}s\le\|f_m\|_{L^2(Q_T)}^2+\|c\|_\infty\|u_m(t)\|_2^2+C\|\varphi_m\|_{H_0^1}^2.
$$
Together with the basic energy estimate, this yields the theorem's bound for $u_m$.

**Step 4. Pass to the limit.** Apply the same bound to $u_m-u_l$ with data $f_m-f_l$ and $\varphi_m-\varphi_l$. The sequence is Cauchy in both $\dot W_2^{1,1}$ and $C_tH_0^1$. Pass to the limit in the weak equation. Finally,
$$
\|u(t)-\varphi\|_{H_0^1}\le\|u(t)-u_m(t)\|_{H_0^1}+\|u_m(t)-\varphi_m\|_{H_0^1}+\|\varphi_m-\varphi\|_{H_0^1}
$$
gives the initial condition in $H_0^1$.
**QED**

## $W_2^{2,1}$ regularity

> [!thm]
> In the special existence theorem, assume additionally $a_{ij}\in C^1(\overline\Omega)$ and $\partial\Omega\in C^2$. Then
> $$
> u\in W_2^{2,1}(Q_T),\qquad\|u\|_{W_2^{2,1}}\le C(\|\varphi\|_{H_0^1}+\|f\|_{L^2(Q_T)}).
> $$

**Proof**
For a.e. $t$, $Lu_m=f_m-(u_m)_t$. [[5. Global H2-Regularity Theory]] gives
$$
\|u_m(t)\|_{H^2}\le C(\|f_m(t)\|_2+\|(u_m)_t(t)\|_2+\|u_m(t)\|_2).
$$
Square, integrate in time, and use the preceding estimate. Applying it to differences shows $u_m$ is Cauchy in $W_2^{2,1}$.
**QED**

## Higher-order $L^2$ regularity

Continue with the same time-independent self-adjoint operator. Let $m\ge1$, $a_{ij}\in C^{2m+1}(\overline\Omega)$, $c\in C^{2m}(\overline\Omega)$, $\partial\Omega\in C^{2m+2}$,
$$
f\in W_2^{2m,m}(Q_T),\qquad\varphi\in H^{2m+1}(\Omega).
$$
The compatibility conditions are
$$
\varphi_0=\varphi\in H_0^1,\qquad\varphi_j=\partial_t^{j-1}f(\cdot,0)-L\varphi_{j-1}\in H_0^1,\quad j=1,\ldots,m.
$$

> [!thm] Global high-order regularity
> Under these assumptions,
> $$
> u\in W_2^{2m+2,m+1}(Q_T),\qquad\|u\|_{W_2^{2m+2,m+1}}\le C(\|f\|_{W_2^{2m,m}}+\|\varphi\|_{H^{2m+1}}).
> $$

**Proof for $m=1$**
**Claim 1.** $d_k=(f,e_k)_2\in H^1(0,T)$ and $d_k'=(f_t,e_k)_2$. Approximate $f$ smoothly in $W_2^{2,1}$; Hölder's inequality gives convergence of the scalar coefficients and their derivatives in $L^2(0,T)$.

Thus $C_k''=d_k'-\lambda_kC_k'\in L^2$. Differentiate the Galerkin equation:
$$
w_m=(u_m)_t,\qquad (w_m)_t+Lw_m=(f_m)_t,\qquad w_m(0)=f_m(0)-L\varphi_m=P_m\varphi_1.
$$
Since $(f_m)_t\to f_t$ in $L^2$ and $P_m\varphi_1\to\varphi_1$ in $H_0^1$, the $W_2^{2,1}$ estimate implies $w_m\to u_t$ in $W_2^{2,1}$ and
$$
\|u_t\|_{W_2^{2,1}}\le C(\|f_t\|_2+\|f(0)-L\varphi\|_{H_0^1}).
$$
Now apply the higher-order elliptic estimate to the limiting equation $Lu=f-u_t$, at a.e. time:
$$
\|u(t)\|_{H^4}\le C(\|f(t)\|_{H^2}+\|u_t(t)\|_{H^2}+\|u(t)\|_2).
$$
Integrating gives $u\in W_2^{4,2}$. The time-trace estimate controls $f(0)$ in $H^1$, and $\|L\varphi\|_{H^1}\le C\|\varphi\|_{H^3}$. Hence
$$
\|u\|_{W_2^{4,2}}\le C(\|f\|_{W_2^{2,1}}+\|\varphi\|_{H^3}).
$$
Iterate for general $m$.
**QED**

> [!note] 整理注：passing to high spatial regularity
> The line on p. 42 applies an $H^4$ estimate to $u_m$ and treats $f_m$ as Cauchy in $H^2$. Dirichlet eigenfunction projections of arbitrary $H^2$ data need not converge in $H^2$ without suitable boundary traces. Applying the elliptic estimate to the limiting equation $Lu=f-u_t$ avoids this gap and preserves the proof's main argument.

> [!remark] Smoothing and local estimates
> 1. Smooth data, coefficients, boundary, and compatibility conditions of all orders imply $u\in C^\infty(\overline{Q_T})$.
> 2. On $Q'\Subset_p Q_T$ (away from the initial and lateral boundary),
> $$
> \|u\|_{W_2^{2m+2,m+1}(Q')}\le C(\|f\|_{W_2^{2m,m}(Q_T)}+\|u\|_{L^2(Q_T)}).
> $$
> 3. With zero lateral data and smooth boundary, for every $\delta>0$,
> $$
> \|u\|_{W_2^{2m+2,m+1}(\Omega\times(\delta,T))}\le C_\delta(\|f\|_{W_2^{2m,m}(Q_T)}+\|u\|_{L^2(Q_T)}).
> $$
> Use a time cutoff $\eta(t)$ vanishing near $0$ and equal to $1$ for $t\ge\delta$.
> 4. Initial data in $H^{2m+1}$ lead to spatial derivatives of order $2m+2$ in the spacetime Sobolev norm: the parabolic smoothing effect.
