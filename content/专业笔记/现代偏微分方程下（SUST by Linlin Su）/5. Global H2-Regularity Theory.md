# Global $H^2$-Regularity Theory

2026.4.8

Let $Lu=-(a_{ij}u_{x_i})_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}+cu$ on a bdd domain $\Omega$.

> [!thm] Global $H^2$ regularity
> Assume $a_{ij}\in C^1(\overline\Omega)$, $b_i,c\in L^\infty(\Omega)$, $\partial\Omega\in C^2$ and uniform ellipticity. If $f\in L^2$ and $u\in H_0^1$ is a weak sol of $Lu=f$, then
> $$u\in H^2(\Omega),\qquad\|u\|_{H^2(\Omega)}\le C(\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}).$$

**Proof**
**Step 1. Localization.** Cover $\partial\Omega$ by finitely many $C^2$ charts and cover the remaining compact interior by $B_0\Subset\Omega$. The [[3. L2-Interior Regularity Theory for second-order Elliptic Equations#Interior $H^2$ estimate|interior estimate]] treats $B_0$. Near a boundary point, choose nested patches $\Omega_1=\Omega\cap B_r$ and $\Omega_2=\Omega\cap B_{2r}$, and a cutoff $\eta=1$ on $B_r$, compactly supported in $B_{2r}$. Let $\overline u=\eta u$. Then
$$
L\overline u=\overline f
=\eta f+b_i\eta_{x_i}u-(a_{ij}\eta_{x_i}u)_{x_j}-\eta_{x_j}a_{ij}u_{x_i},
$$
and
$$\|\overline f\|_{L^2(\Omega_2)}\le C(\|f\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{H^1(\Omega)}).$$

**Step 2. Flattening.** Use the [[4. Global Regularity#Boundary charts|boundary chart]] $y=\Phi(x)$ and set $\widetilde u=\overline u\circ\Phi^{-1}$. The weak equation becomes
$$\int_{N_2^+}(a^*_{k\ell}\widetilde u_{y_k}\widetilde v_{y_\ell}
+b_k^*\widetilde u_{y_k}\widetilde v+c^*\widetilde u\widetilde v){d}y
=\int_{N_2^+}\widetilde f\widetilde v\,{d}y,$$
where
$$
a^*_{k\ell}=\left(a_{ij}\frac{\partial y_k}{\partial x_i}\frac{\partial y_\ell}{\partial x_j}\right)\circ\Phi^{-1},
\quad b_k^*=\left(b_i\frac{\partial y_k}{\partial x_i}\right)\circ\Phi^{-1},
\quad c^*=c\circ\Phi^{-1},\quad\widetilde f=\overline f\circ\Phi^{-1}.
$$
Writing $J_{ik}=\partial y_k/\partial x_i$, $A^*=J^TAJ$. Consequently
$$\xi^TA^*\xi\ge\lambda_0|J\xi|^2\ge\frac{\lambda_0}{\|J^{-1}\|^2}|\xi|^2.$$
Thus $A^*$ remains symmetric and uniformly elliptic, with $C^1$ coefficients. The localized function has zero trace on the flat part and vanishes near the curved part of the patch boundary.

**Step 3. Tangential derivatives.** For $k=1,\ldots,n-1$, tangential shifts preserve the flat boundary condition. Test using $-\nabla_k^{-h}\nabla_k^h\widetilde u$ (with an additional cutoff if necessary). The interior difference-quotient argument gives
$$\|\nabla_k^hD\widetilde u\|_{L^2(N_1^+)}\le C(\|\widetilde f\|_{L^2(N_2^+)}+\|\widetilde u\|_{H^1(N_2^+)}).$$
Hence all $\widetilde u_{y_ky_i}$ with $k<n$, $i\le n$, belong to $L^2$ with this bound.

**Step 4. The second normal derivative.** Interior regularity already makes the equation valid a.e. in $N_2^+$. Solve it for $\widetilde u_{y_ny_n}$:
$$
\widetilde u_{y_ny_n}=\frac1{a_{nn}^*}\left[
\sum_k b_k^*\widetilde u_{y_k}+c^*\widetilde u-\widetilde f
-\sum_{k,\ell}(a^*_{k\ell})_{y_\ell}\widetilde u_{y_k}
-\sum_{(k,\ell)\ne(n,n)}a^*_{k\ell}\widetilde u_{y_ky_\ell}
\right].
$$
The right-hand side is in $L^2$ and $a_{nn}^*$ is bounded below. This proves the boundary $H^2$ estimate.

**Step 5. Global estimate.** Transform back, sum over the finite cover, and obtain
$$\|u\|_{H^2}\le C(\|f\|_2+\|u\|_{H^1}).$$
Use [[4. Global Regularity#Interpolation inequalities]]:
$$\|u\|_{H^1}\le\varepsilon\|u\|_{H^2}+C_\varepsilon\|u\|_2,$$
and absorb the first term for small $\varepsilon$.
**QED**

## Higher-order global regularity

2026.4.15

> [!thm]
> Let $m\ge0$ be an integer. Assume $\partial\Omega\in C^{m+2}$, $a_{ij}\in C^{m+1}(\overline\Omega)$, $b_i,c\in C^m(\overline\Omega)$ and $f\in H^m(\Omega)$. A weak Dirichlet sol satisfies
> $$u\in H^{m+2}(\Omega),\qquad
> \|u\|_{H^{m+2}}\le C(\|f\|_{H^m}+\|u\|_2).$$
> For $m=0$, $b_i,c\in L^\infty$ suffices.

> [!note] 整理注
> The statement on p. 24 omits the higher-order boundary assumption. The proof uses boundary flattening at the same order; $\partial\Omega\in C^{m+2}$ is included here.

**Proof sketch in the manuscript**
The case $m=0$ is the previous theorem. For $m=1$, localize and flatten the boundary. In the flattened coordinates, tangential derivatives $u_{x_k}$, $k<n$, have zero trace and satisfy
$$L(u_{x_k})=f_{x_k}+\big((a_{ij})_{x_k}u_{x_i}\big)_{x_j}-(b_i)_{x_k}u_{x_i}-c_{x_k}u\in L^2.$$
Apply the boundary $H^2$ estimate to these derivatives. This controls all third derivatives containing a tangential differentiation. Rearranging the original equation for $u_{x_nx_n}$ then shows $u_{x_nx_n}\in H^1$ and controls $u_{x_nx_nx_n}$.

To justify zero trace of $u_{x_k}$, use the localized function whose support avoids the curved boundary. For small tangential shifts,
$$\nabla_k^hu\in H_0^1(N^+),\qquad
\|\nabla_k^hu\|_{H^1}\le C\|u\|_{H^2}.$$
A weakly convergent subsequence in $H_0^1$ has limit $u_{x_k}$, identified by discrete integration by parts. Induct on $m$.
**QED**

> [!corollary]
> If the coefficients, $f$, and $\partial\Omega$ are smooth, then $u\in C^\infty(\overline\Omega)$.

> [!note] 整理注
> Smoothness of $f$ is necessary in this corollary and was omitted from its line on p. 26.

## De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theorem

The manuscript records the following results without proof. Here $a_{ij},b_i,c\in L^\infty$, $L$ is uniformly elliptic, and $u\in H^1$ solves $Lu=f$, with $f\in L^q$, $q>n/2$ (in the usual range $q>1$).

> [!thm]
> 1. For $\Omega'\Subset\Omega$, there are $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $C$ s.t.
> $$u\in C^\alpha(\overline{\Omega'}),\qquad
> \|u\|_{C^\alpha(\overline{\Omega'})}\le C(\|u\|_{L^2(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^q(\Omega)}).$$
> 2. If $u\in H_0^1(\Omega)$, then
> $$\|u\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le C(\|u\|_2+\|f\|_q).$$
> 3. If also $\partial\Omega\in C^2$, then
> $$u\in C^\alpha(\overline\Omega),\qquad\|u\|_{C^\alpha(\overline\Omega)}\le C(\|u\|_2+\|f\|_q).$$

## Neumann / Robin BVP

Let $\nu$ be the outer normal and $\partial_{\nu_A}u=(A\nu)\cdot Du$. Consider
$$\begin{cases}Lu=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\\partial_{\nu_A}u+\beta u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}$$
For bounded $\beta$ and a $C^1$ boundary, the weak formulation is $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ and
$$\int_\Omega(a_{ij}u_{x_i}v_{x_j}+b_i u_{x_i}v+cuv){d}x
+\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta(Tu)(Tv){d}S=\int_\Omega fv\,{d}x,\quad\forall v\in H^1(\Omega).$$
The trace term is controlled by [[4. Global Regularity#Trace theorem]]. The Fredholm alternative says that uniqueness for the homogeneous problem implies existence and uniqueness for all admissible right-hand sides. Pure Neumann problems may have a nontrivial kernel; uniqueness is an assumption here.
