# Schauder Theory for Parabolic Eqs.

2026.5.27 · [[8. L2-theory for parabolic equations]]

Consider the uniformly parabolic operator
$$
\mathscr Lu=u_t-a_{ij}(x,t)u_{x_ix_j}+b_i(x,t)u_{x_i}+c(x,t)u=u_t+L(t)u
$$
and IBVP
$$
\mathscr Lu=f\text{ in }Q_T,\qquad u=0\text{ on }S_T,\qquad u(\cdot,0)=\varphi.
$$

## Anisotropic Hölder spaces

> [!def] Parabolic distance and Hölder seminorm
> For $P=(x,t)$ and $Q=(y,s)$,
> $$
> d(P,Q)=(|x-y|^2+|t-s|)^{1/2},\qquad[u]_{\alpha,\alpha/2;\overline{Q_T}}=
> \sup_{P\ne Q}\frac{|u(P)-u(Q)|}{d(P,Q)^\alpha}.
> $$
> The space $C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{Q_T})$ is Banach with norm
> $$
> \|u\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}}=\|u\|_{C^0}+[u]_{\alpha,\alpha/2}.
> $$

For $k\ge0$, $C^{2k,k}$ consists of functions with continuous derivatives $\partial_x^\beta\partial_t^\gamma u$ for $|\beta|+2\gamma\le2k$, with the sum of their sup norms. The manuscript writes
$$
C^{2k+\alpha,k+\alpha/2}=\{u\in C^{2k,k}:\partial_x^\beta\partial_t^\gamma u\in C^{\alpha,\alpha/2},\ |\beta|+2\gamma\le2k\},
$$
and uses the norm
$$
\|u\|_{C^{2k+\alpha,k+\alpha/2}}=\|u\|_{C^{2k,k}}+\sum_{|\beta|+2\gamma\le2k}[\partial_x^\beta\partial_t^\gamma u]_{\alpha,\alpha/2}.
$$

## Sobolev-Hölder embedding

Recall the elliptic rule: if $k-n/p>0$ is not an integer, $W^{k,p}\hookrightarrow C^{k-n/p}$; at an integer threshold one takes a smaller Hölder order.

In parabolic scaling, $n$ is replaced by $n+2$. On a domain admitting the required bounded extension, if
$$
s=2k-\frac{n+2}{p}>0,\qquad s\notin\mathbb{N},
$$
then
$$
W_p^{2k,k}(Q_T)\hookrightarrow C^{s,s/2}(\overline{Q_T}).
$$
At critical integer exponents, use any strictly smaller noninteger exponent.

## Existence, uniqueness and compatibility

> [!thm] Global Schauder theory
> Let $k\ge0$, $0<\alpha<1$. Assume
> $$
> \partial\Omega\in C^{2k+2+\alpha},\quad a_{ij},b_i,c,f\in C^{2k+\alpha,k+\alpha/2}(\overline{Q_T}),\quad\varphi\in C^{2k+2+\alpha}(\overline\Omega),
> $$
> uniform parabolicity, and the compatibility conditions through time order $k+1$. Then IBVP has a unique sol
> $$
> u\in C^{2k+2+\alpha,k+1+\alpha/2}(\overline{Q_T}),
> $$
> satisfying
> $$
> \|u\|_{C^{2k+2+\alpha,k+1+\alpha/2}}\le C(\|f\|_{C^{2k+\alpha,k+\alpha/2}}+\|\varphi\|_{C^{2k+2+\alpha}}).
> $$

The compatibility conditions begin with
$$
\varphi=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\qquad u_t(\cdot,0)=f(\cdot,0)-L(0)\varphi=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,
$$
and continue with $\partial_t^ju(\cdot,0)=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, $j\le k+1$, computed recursively from the equation. If the coefficients depend on $t$, differentiating introduces their time derivatives as well.

> [!remark]
> Unlike the elliptic stationary problem, a finite-time parabolic IBVP does not require $c\ge0$ for uniqueness; a bounded $c$ can be handled by an exponential time factor or energy estimate.

## Interior and boundary Schauder estimates

Let $\Gamma=\overline{S_T}\cup(\overline\Omega\times\{0\})$ be the parabolic boundary; the terminal face is not an initial-data boundary.

> [!thm] Interior estimate
> If $a_{ij},b_i,c,f\in C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}$ and $\mathscr Lu=f$, then, on a closed spacetime subregion $Q'$ separated from $\Gamma$,
> $$
> \|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(Q')}\le C(\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{Q_T})}+\|u\|_{C^0(\overline{Q_T})}).
> $$
> The constant depends on the coefficients, ellipticity, geometry and the distance to the parabolic boundary.

> [!thm] Boundary estimate away from $t=0$
> If also $\partial\Omega\in C^{2+\alpha}$ and $u=0$ on $S_T$, then for $0<\varepsilon<T$,
> $$
> \|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(\overline\Omega\times[\varepsilon,T])}\le C_\varepsilon(\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\overline{Q_T})}+\|u\|_{C^0(\overline{Q_T})}).
> $$

The manuscript states these Schauder results without proof. They are used in [[7. Existence and Uniqueness of nonlinear heat equations]].
