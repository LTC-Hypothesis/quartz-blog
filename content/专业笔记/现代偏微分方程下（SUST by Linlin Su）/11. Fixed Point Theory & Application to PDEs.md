# Fixed Point Theory & Application to PDEs

2026.6.3

Let $X$ be a Banach space.

## Schauder's fixed point theorem

> [!thm] Schauder
> Let $K\subset X$ be nonempty, compact and convex. If $T:K\to K$ is continuous, there exists $x\in K$ s.t. $Tx=x$.

Recall Brouwer's theorem: a continuous self-map of a closed ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has a fixed point; equivalently one may use a nonempty compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional space. In one dimension, $T:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ gives $f(x)=x-T(x)$ with $f(0)\le0$ and $f(1)\ge0$, so the intermediate value theorem supplies a zero in $[0,1]$.

**Proof of Schauder's theorem**
For each $n$, compactness gives a finite cover
$$
K\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{M(n)}B_{1/n}(x_i),\qquad x_i\in K.
$$
Let
$$
K_n=\operatorname{co}\{x_1,\ldots,x_{M(n)}\}\subset K.
$$
This is a compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional space. Define nonnegative continuous weights and an approximate projection by
$$
\rho_i(x)=\max\{1/n-\|x-x_i\|,0\},\qquad J_n(x)=\frac{\sum_i\rho_i(x)x_i}{\sum_i\rho_i(x)}.
$$
The denominator is positive on $K$, and $J_n:K\to K_n$ is continuous. Only centers with $\|x-x_i\|<1/n$ have nonzero weight, so
$$
\|J_n(x)-x\|\le\frac{\sum_i\rho_i(x)\|x_i-x\|}{\sum_i\rho_i(x)}<\frac1n.
$$
By Brouwer, $J_n\circ T:K_n\to K_n$ has a fixed point $x_n$. Passing to a subsequence, $x_n\to x_\infty\in K$. Then
$$
\|Tx_n-x_n\|=\|Tx_n-J_n(Tx_n)\|<1/n,
$$
and continuity gives $Tx_\infty=x_\infty$.
**QED**

> [!note] 整理注
> The manuscript uses weights $\operatorname{dist}(x,K\setminus B_i)$. The weights above have the same support and approximation property, and avoid the empty-complement case when a ball contains all of $K$. Also, Brouwer's one-dimensional fixed point may be an endpoint, so the interval is $[0,1]$, not necessarily $(0,1)$.

## A compact-image version

> [!corollary]
> Let $K\subset X$ be nonempty, closed and convex. If $T:K\to K$ is continuous and $T(K)$ is precompact, then $T$ has a fixed point.

**Proof**
Use the fact that the closed convex hull of a compact subset of a Banach space is compact. Put
$$
M=\overline{\operatorname{co}}\big(\overline{T(K)}\big).
$$
Since $K$ is closed and convex, $M\subset K$. Also $T(M)\subset T(K)\subset M$. Apply Schauder to $T|_M$.
**QED**

## Schaefer's theorem

> [!thm] Schaefer
> Let $T:X\to X$ be continuous and compact (it maps bounded sets to relatively compact sets). If
> $$
> S=\{x\in X:x=\lambda Tx\text{ for some }0\le\lambda\le1\}
> $$
> is bounded, then $T$ has a fixed point.

The manuscript states this theorem without proof.

## Application to a nonlinear elliptic equation

Consider
$$
\begin{cases}
-\Delta u+b(Du)+\mu u=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}\tag{DBVP}
$$
where $\Omega$ is a bdd $C^2$ domain, $b:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ is smooth, and
$$
|b(z)|\le C(1+|z|).
$$

> [!thm]
> For sufficiently large $\mu$, DBVP has a sol $u\in H^2(\Omega)\cap H_0^1(\Omega)$.

**Proof**
Set $X=H_0^1(\Omega)$. For $u\in X$, linear growth gives $b(Du)\in L^2$. Define $Tu=w$ by
$$
\begin{cases}
-\Delta w+\mu w=-b(Du)&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
\end{cases}
$$
For $\mu>0$, [[1. Existence and Uniqueness of weak solutions of elliptic equations]] and [[5. Global H2-Regularity Theory]] give a unique $w\in H^2\cap H_0^1$.

**Step 1. Continuity.** If $u_k\to u$ in $H_0^1$, then $Du_k\to Du$ in $L^2$. Continuity and linear growth of $b$ imply $b(Du_k)\to b(Du)$ in $L^2$. One justification is convergence in measure plus uniform integrability of $|b(Du_k)-b(Du)|^2$, obtained from the strong $L^2$ convergence of $Du_k$ and the growth bound. This does not require a globally bounded derivative of $b$.

The resolvent estimate gives
$$
\|Tu_k-Tu\|_{H^2}\le C_\mu\|b(Du_k)-b(Du)\|_2\to0.
$$
Thus $T$ is continuous as a map into $X$.

**Step 2. Compactness.** If $(u_k)$ is bounded in $X$, then
$$
\|Tu_k\|_{H^2}\le C_\mu\|b(Du_k)\|_2\le C_\mu(1+\|Du_k\|_2).
$$
Since $H^2(\Omega)\cap H_0^1(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H_0^1(\Omega)$ compactly, $(Tu_k)$ has a convergent subsequence in $X$.

**Step 3. A priori bound for the Schaefer set.** If $u=\lambda Tu$, $0\le\lambda\le1$, then
$$
-\Delta u+\mu u=-\lambda b(Du),\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.
$$
Test with $u$ and use Young's inequality:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|Du\|_2^2+\mu\|u\|_2^2
&=-\lambda\int_\Omega b(Du)u\,{d}x\\
&\le C\int_\Omega(1+|Du|)|u|\,{d}x\\
&\le C\varepsilon\|Du\|_2^2+C_\varepsilon\|u\|_2^2+C.
\end{aligned}
$$
Choose $\varepsilon$ with $C\varepsilon<1$, then choose $\mu>C_\varepsilon$. This gives a bound for $\|u\|_{H_0^1}$ independent of $\lambda$. Schaefer supplies $u=Tu$, which belongs to $H^2\cap H_0^1$ and solves DBVP.
**QED**

> [!remark]
> The argument proves existence. It does not assert uniqueness for the nonlinear problem.

See also [[7. Existence and Uniqueness of nonlinear heat equations]] for comparison and blow-up methods.
