# Two Notions of Uniqueness
> [!def] Weak solution
> A weak solution of [[Strong solutions#^53e799|(SDE2)]] is a triple $(X,W),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$, which satisfies
> 1. $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ is a probability space and $\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$ is a sub-$\sigma$ algebra of $\mathcal{F}$ satisfying the usual condition. 
> 2. $X_t$ is a continuous, adapted process and $W_t$ is a r-dimensional Brownian motion.
> 3. It holds that 
>    $$
>    \mathbb{P}\left[\int_{0}^{t}|b_i(s,X_s)|^2+\sigma_{ij}^2(s,X_s){d}s<\infty\right]=1,1\le i\le d,1\le j\le r,0\le t<\infty
>    $$
> 4. It holds that 
>    $$
>    X_t=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_s){d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_s){d}W_s,a.s.-\mathbb{P}
>    $$
>    
> The initial condition is the same as [[Strong solutions#^32ec87|the strong solution]]. 

> [!warning] Difference between strong solution and weak solution
> The difference between strong solution and weak solution is the filtration $\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$, which is not necessarily [[Strong solutions#^32ec87|generated by the Brownian motion and the initial condition]]. Thus, $X_t$ is not necessarily given by $W_s$ and $\xi$. In other words, $X_t(\omega)$ cannot anticipate the future of Brownian motion $W_t$. 

> [!def] Pathwise uniqueness
> Suppose that $(X,W),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$ and $(\tilde{X},W),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),\{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_t\}$ are weak solutions of [[Strong solutions#^53e799|(SDE2)]]. We say they are **pathwise uniqueness** if 
> 1. They holds [[Strong solutions#^53e799|(SDE2)]] with the common Brownian motion $W_t$, probability space and initial codnition $\mathbb{P}[X_0=\tilde{X}_t;0\le t<\infty]=1$.
> 2. They are indistinguishable i.e. 
>    $$
>    \mathbb{P}[X_t=\tilde{X}_t;0\le t<\infty]=1
>    $$

> [!def] Uniqueness of probability law
> We say the two weak solutions $(X,W),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$ and $(\tilde{X},\tilde{W}),(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}}),\{\tilde{\mathcal{F}_t}\}$ if they holds [[Strong solutions#^53e799|(SDE2)]] with the same initial condition i.e. 
> $$
> \mathbb{P}[X_0\in\Gamma]=\tilde{\mathbb{P}}[X_0\in\Gamma],\forall\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d)
> $$
> and the two process $X,\tilde{X}$ have the same law.

> [!example] Tanaka
> Consider the 1-dimensional SDE
> $$
> X_t=\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}(X_s){d}W_s\tag{T}
> $$
> where 
> $$
> \text{sign}(x)=\begin{cases}
> 1,&x>0\\
> -1,&x\le 0
> \end{cases}
> $$

> [!warning] Moral story of Tanaka's example
> - Existence of weak solution $\nRightarrow$ existence of the strong solution.
> - Uniqueness of probability law $\nRightarrow$ pathwise uniqueness. 

**Solution**
Suppose $(X,W),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}),\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$ is a weak solution of SDE ($\text{T}$) which belong $\mathcal{M}_2^c$ with the quadratic variation process 
$$
\langle X\rangle_t=\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}^2(X_s){d}s=t
$$
By [[The Change-of-Variable Formula#^b655c5|Charateristic of Brownian motion]], $X_t$ is a Brownian motion and thus uniqueness of probability law. Note that $(-X,W)$ is also the weak solution of SDE ($\text{T}$) and $X\ne -X,a.s.-\mathbb{P}$ obviously. Hence, uniqueness of probability law $\nRightarrow$ pathwise uniqueness.
Now we construct the weak solution of SDE ($\text{T}$). Suppose a probability space and a one-dimensional Brownian motion $X=\{X_t,\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^{X}_t\}$.
Assume that $\mathbb{P}[X_0=0]=1$ and denote by $\mathring{\mathcal{F}}$ e augmentation of the filtration. We construct
$$
W_t\triangleq\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}(X_s){d}X_s\Longrightarrow\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}(X_s){d}W_s=\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}^2(X_s){d}X_s=X_t
$$
and note that $\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^W_t\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^W_t$ which is the difference between strong solution and weak solution.
In order to prove nonexistance of strong solution, we need Tanaka's formula,  
$$
2L_t(a)=|W_t-a|-|z-a|-\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}(W_s-a){d}W_s,\forall z\in\mathbb{R}
$$

^a00352

where $L_t(a,\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\delta(W_s(\omega)-a){d}s$. We assume that $X$ holds the SDE ($\text{T}$) on given probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ w.r.t. Brownian motion $W_t$ and $\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^W_t\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^W_t$. By Tanaka's formula, We obtain
$$
\begin{align}
W_t&=\int_{0}^{t}\text{sign}(X_s){d}X_s=|X_t|-2L^X_t(0)\\
&=|X_t|-\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\text{Leb}\left\{0\le s\le t:|X_s|\le \varepsilon\right\}
\end{align}
$$
The subtle point is that the local time $L_t(0)$ is determined by how $X_t$​ hits and accumulates time at zero. However, the absolute value $|X_t|$ alone does not uniquely determine this local time: there are infinitely many possible continuous paths that have the same absolute value but differ in how often or how long they touch zero. Consequently, $\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^W_t\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^{|X|}_t\Longrightarrow\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^X_t\subseteq\mathring{\mathcal{F}}^{|X|}_t$ is a contradiction!
**QED**
# Weak Solutions by Means of the Girsanov Theorem
In this section, we will construct the weak solution by **transformation of drift** via the Girsanov theorem. 
> [!proposition]
> Consider the SDE 
> $$
> dX_t=b(t,X_t){d}t+dW_t,0\le t\le T\tag{G}
> $$
> where $T>0$, $b:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is Borel-measurable and satisfies
> $$
> \left\|b(t,x)\right\|\le K(1+\|x\|)
> $$
> Then for any probability measure $\mu$ on $(\mathbb{R}^d,\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d))$, SDE (G) has a weak solution with initial distribution $\mu$.

**Proof**
Consider the Brownian family $X,(\Omega,\mathcal{F}),\{\mathbb{P}\}^x$. By [[The Girsanov Theorem#^884ad1|the Girsanov Theorem (*1)]], we have 
$$
\begin{align}
Z_t\triangleq\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}b_j(s,X_s){d}X_s^{(j)}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|b(s,X_s)\|^2{d}s\right)
\end{align}
$$
is a martingale and by [[The Girsanov Theorem#^db25d3|the Girsanov theorem]], 
$$
W_t\triangleq X_t-x_0-\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_s){d}s
$$
is Brownian motion with $Q^x[W_0=0]=1$. We rewrite as 
$$
X_t=x_0+\int_{0}^{t}b(s,X_s){d}s+W_t
$$
We should prove $X_0\sim\mu$ but we only have $Q^x[X_0=x_0]=1$. We define
$$
Q^\mu(X_0\in\Gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}Q^x(X_0\in\Gamma)\mu({d}x)
$$
Hence, $(X,W),(\Omega,\mathcal{F},Q^\mu),\{\mathcal{F}_t\}$ is a weak solution of SDE (G).
**QED**
> [!warning]
> - The conclusion can be extended to include the case $b(t,x)$ is progressively measurable functional on $C[0,\infty)^d$. 
> - When the initial distribution $\mu$ degenerates to unit point mass at some $x\in\mathbb{R}^d$, the filtration $\{\mathcal{F}^W_t\}$ may be strictly smaller than the filtration $\{\mathcal{F}^X_t\}$.

> $$
> \mathbb{P}^{(i)}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|b(t,X_t^{(i)})\|^2{d}t<\infty\right]=1
> $$
> then $(X^{(i)},W^{(i)}),i=1,2$ has the same law under respective probability measures.

**Proof**
For $k\ge1$, we define
$$
\tau^{(i)}_k=T\wedge \inf\left\{0\le t\le T:\int_{0}^{t}\|b(s,X^{(i)}_s)\|^2{d}s=k\right\}
$$
By [[The Girsanov Theorem#^01516a|Novikov condition]], we have 
$$
\xi^{(k)}_t(X^{(i)})=\exp\left\{-\int_{0}^{t\wedge \tau_k^{(i)}}b(s,X_s^{(i)}){d}W^{(i)}_s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge \tau_k^{(i)}}\|b(s,X^{(i)}_s)\|^2{d}s\right\}
$$
is a martingale under the probability measure $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_k^{(i)}$, which satisfies 
$$
\frac{d\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_k^{(i)}}{d\mathbb{P}^{(i)}}=\xi^{(k)}_T(X^{(i)})
$$
By [[The Girsanov Theorem#^db25d3|Girsanov theorem]], we obtain 
$$
X^{(i)}_{t\wedge \tau^{(i)}_k}=x^{(i)}_0+\int_{0}^{t\wedge \tau^{(i)}_k}b(s,X^{(i)}_s){d}s+W^{(i)}_{t\wedge \tau^{(i)}_k}
$$
is a Brownian motion under probability measure $\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{(i)}_k$ with initial measure $\mu$ and stopping at $\tau^{(i)}_k$. Recall [[The Girsanov Theorem#^6b9f7f|the form of Ito integral under the probability measure changed]], it implies that $t\wedge \tau^{(i)}_k,W_t,\xi_t^{(k)}(X^{(i)})$ can be defined in the same form and thus they have the same distribution. Then for $\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2d(n+1)})$ and $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=T$
$$
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}^{(1)}\left[(X^1_{t_0},W_{t_0}^1,\cdots,X^1_{t_n},W_{t_n}^1)\in\Gamma\right]&=\int_{\Omega^{(1)}}\mathbb{1}_{\{(X^1_{t_0},W_{t_0}^1,\cdots,X^1_{t_n},W_{t_n}^1,)\in\Gamma\}}{d}\mathbb{P}^{(1)}\\
&=\int_{\Omega^{(1)}}\frac{1}{\xi^{(k)}_t(X^{(1)})}\mathbb{1}_{\{(X^1_{t_0},W_{t_0}^1,\cdots,X^1_{t_n},W_{t_n}^1)\in\Gamma\}}{d}\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{(1)}_k\\
&=\int_{\Omega^{(2)}}\frac{1}{\xi^{(k)}_t(X^{(2)})}\mathbb{1}_{\{(X^2_{t_0},W_{t_0}^2,\cdots,X^2_{t_n},W_{t_n}^2)\in\Gamma\}}{d}\tilde{\mathbb{P}}^{(2)}_k\\
&=\mathbb{P}^{(2)}\left[(X^2_{t_0},W_{t_0}^2,\cdots,X^2_{t_n},W_{t_n}^2)\in\Gamma\right]
\end{align}
$$
**QED**

# A Digression on Regular Conditional Probabilities
> [!def] Regular conditional probability 
> Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ be a probability space and $\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}$ be sub $\sigma$-field. Let the mapping $Q:\Omega\times\mathcal{F}\to[0,1]$, we say it is regular conditional probability for $\mathbb{F}$ for given $\mathcal{G}$, if 
> 1. For fixed $\omega\in\Omega$, $Q(\omega,\cdot):\mathcal{F}\to[0,1]$ is a probability measure
> 2. For fixed $A\in\mathcal{F}$, $\omega\mapsto Q(\omega,A)$ is $\mathcal{G}$-measurable. 
> 3. For each $A\in\mathcal{F}$, $Q(\omega,A)=\mathbb{P}[A|\mathcal{G}]$

> [!def] Uniqueness of regular conditional probability
> We say the regular conditional probability is unique if there exists another measure $Q'(\omega,A)$ s.t. for any $A\in\mathcal{F}$ and $\omega\in\Omega\setminus N$ where $N$ has probability 0, it holds that $Q'(\omega,A)=Q(\omega,A)$.

> [!thm] User friendly conclusion of regular conditional probability
> Let $(\Omega,\mathscr{F},P)$ be a probability space, and let $X$ be a measurable mapping from this space into a measurable space $(S,\mathscr{S})$, on which it induces the distribution $PX^{-1}(B)\triangleq P[\omega\in\Omega;\,X(\omega)\in B],\;B\in\mathscr{S}$. There exists then a function $Q(x;A):S\times\mathscr{F}\to[0,1]$, called a regular conditional probability for $\mathscr{F}$ given $X$, s.t. 
> 4. For each $x\in S$, $Q(x;\cdot)$ is a probability measure on $(\Omega,\mathscr{F})$, 
> 5. For each $A\in\mathscr{F}$, the mapping $x\mapsto Q(x;A)$ is $\mathscr{S}$-measurable, 
> 6. For each $A\in\mathscr{F}$, $Q(x;A)=P[A\mid X=x],\;PX^{-1}$-a.e. $x\in S$. 
>    
> If $Q'(x;A)$ is another function with these properties, then there exists a set $N\in\mathscr{S}$ with $PX^{-1}(N)=0$ such that $Q(x;A)=Q'(x;A)$ for all $A\in\mathscr{F}$ and $x\in S\setminus N$. Furthermore, if $S$ is also a complete, separable metric space and $\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{B}(S)$, then $N$ can be chosen so that we have the additional property: $$ Q(x;\{\omega\in\Omega;\,X(\omega)\in B\})=\mathbf{1}_B(x);\quad B\in\mathscr{S},\,x\in S\setminus N. $$ In particular, $$Q(x;\{\omega\in\Omega;\,X(\omega)=x\})=1;\quad PX^{-1}\text{-a.e. }x\in S. $$

# Results of Yamada & Watanabe on  Weak and Strong Solutions
> [!thm] Yamada & Watanabe
> Pathwise uniqueness$\Longrightarrow$uniqueness of probability law.

**Proof**
**Step1**
Suppose two weak solution $(X^{(i)},W^{(i)}),(\Omega_i,\mathcal{F}_i,\nu_i),\{\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_t\},i=1,2$ satisfy SDE
$$
dX_t=b(t,X_t){d}t+\sigma(t,X_t){d}W_t
$$
with the same initial distribution i.e. $\forall B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d)$, 
$$
\mu(B)=\nu_1(X^{(1)}_0\in B)=\nu_2(X^{(2)}_0\in B)
$$
We set $Y^{(i)}_t=X^{(i)}_t-X^{(i)}_0$ and thus the weak solutions consistent 3 parts: $X_0^{(i)},W^{(i)}_t,Y^{(i)}$ which induce the probability measure $\mathbb{P}_i$ on $(\Theta,\mathcal{B}(\Theta))$ where $\Theta=\mathbb{R}^d\times C[0,\infty)^r\times C[0,\infty)^d$. For $A\in\mathcal{B}(\Theta)$, $\mathbb{P}_i[A]=\nu_i[(X^{(i)}_0,W^{(i)}_t,Y^{(i)}_t)\in A]$.We denote the element of $\Theta$ by $\theta=(x,w,y)$ where $x$-coordinate is with the initial measure $\mu$ and $w$-coordinate is with the Wiener measure $\mathbb{P}^*$. 
**Step2**
Since the weak solutions $(X^{(i)},W^{(i)})$ are in different probability space, so we should bring them to the same space s.t. they have the same distribution. For probability space $(\Theta,\mathcal{B}(\Theta),\mathbb{P}_i)$, by [[#^0979f3|regular conditional probability theorem]], there exists regular conditional probability measure $Q_i$. For given $(x,w),F\in\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^d)$, $Q_i(x,w,F):\mathbb{R}^d\times C[0,\infty)^r\times C[0,\infty)^d\to[0,1]$ satisfies

1. Fix $x,w$, $Q_i(x,w,\cdot)$ is a probability measure on $(C[0,\infty)^d,\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^d))$
2. Fix $F\in\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^d)$, the mapping $(x,w)\mapsto Q_i(x,w,F)$ is $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d)\otimes\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^d)$-measure
3. For $G\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d)\otimes\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^r),F\in\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^d)$, $$\mathbb{P}_i(G\times F)=\int_{G}Q_i(x,w,F)\mu(dx)\mathbb{P}^*(dw)$$

**Step3**
We consider the probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F}),\Omega=\Theta\times C[0,\infty)^d,\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(\Theta)\otimes\mathcal{B}(C[0,\infty)^d)$ and probability measure $\mathbb{P}(d\omega)\triangleq Q_1(x,w,dy_1)Q_2(x,w,dy_2)\mu(dx)\mathbb{P}^*(dw)$. We denote the element of $\Omega$ by $\omega=(x,w,y_1,y_2)$. The filtration is 
$$
\mathcal{G}_t=\sigma\left\{(x,w,y_1,y_2):0\le s\le t\right\},\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_t=\sigma\left(\mathcal{G}_t\cup\mathcal{N}\right),\mathcal{F}_t\triangleq\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{t+}
$$
The probability measure $\mathbb{P}$ is 
$$
\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\Theta),\mathbb{P}[\omega\in\Omega|(x,w,y_i)\in A]=\nu_i[(X^{(i)}_0,W^{(i)}_t,Y^{(i)}_t)\in A]
$$
Then the distribution of $(x+y_i,w)$ under $\mathbb{P}$ are the same as that of $(X^{(i)},W^{(i)})$ under $\nu_i$
**Step4**
Since $(X^{(i)},W^{(i)})$ are pathwise uniqueness, then 
$$
\mathbb{P}[x+y_1=x+y_2;0\le t<\infty]=1\Longleftrightarrow\mathbb{P}[\omega\in\Omega;y_1=y_2]=1
$$
Then 
$$
\begin{align}
\nu_1[(X^{(1)}_0,W^{(1)}_t,Y^{(1)}_t)\in A]&=\mathbb{P}[\omega\in\Omega;(x,w,y_1)\in A]\\
&=\mathbb{P}[\omega\in\Omega;(x,w,y_2)\in A]\\
&=\nu_2[(X^{(1)}_0,W^{(1)}_t,Y^{(1)}_t)\in A]
\end{align}
$$
**QED**
> [!proposition] Weak solution + pathwise uniqueness $\Longrightarrow$ strong solution
> Suppose that the (SDE2) has a weak solution $(X, W), (\Omega, \mathscr{F}, P), \{\mathscr{F}_t\}$ with initial distribution $\mu$, and suppose that pathwise uniqueness holds for (SDE2). Then there exists a $\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^d)\otimes \mathscr{B}(C[0,\infty)^r)/\mathscr{B}(C[0,\infty)^d)$-measurable function $h: \mathbb{R}^d \times C[0,\infty)^r \to C[0,\infty)^d$, which is also $\hat{\mathscr{B}}_t/\mathscr{B}_t(C[0,\infty)^d)$-measurable for every fixed $0 \le t < \infty$, such that 
> $$ 
> X_{\cdot}= h(X_0, W_{\cdot}),\quad \text{a.s. } P. 
> $$ 
> Moreover, given any probability space $(\tilde\Omega, \tilde{\mathscr{F}}, \tilde P)$ rich enough to support an $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued random variable $\xi$ with distribution $\mu$ and an independent Brownian motion $\{\tilde W_t, \mathscr{F}_t^{\tilde W}; 0 \le t < \infty\}$, the process 
> $$
> \tilde X_{\cdot} \triangleq h(\xi, \tilde W_{\cdot}) 
> $$ 
> is a strong solution of equation (SDE2) with initial condition $\xi$.
