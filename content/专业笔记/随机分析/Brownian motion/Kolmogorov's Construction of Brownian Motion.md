# Introduction of Brownian Motion

> [!def] Brownian motion
> A standard, one-dimensional Brownian motion is a continuous, adapted process $\{B_t,\mathcal{F}_t,0\le t<\infty\}$ defined on some probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ with the properties
> 1. $B_0=0$
> 2. $B_t-B_s$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_s$ for $0\le s<t$
> 3. $B_t-B_s\sim N(0,t-s)$
> 
> **Stationary, independent increment**: for $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n<\infty$, then the increment $B_{t_j}-B_{t_{j-1}}$ are independent and the distribution $B_{t_j}-B_{t_{j-1}}\sim N(0,t_j-t_{j-1})$ only depends on $t_j-t_{j-1}$.
> **quadratic variation**: $B_t$ is a square-integrable martingale and $\langle B\rangle_t=t,t\ge0$ 

> [!def] Dynkin system
> A collection $\mathcal{D}$ of subsets of $\Omega$ s called a Dynkin system if
> 4. $\Omega\in\mathcal{D}$
> 5. $A,B\in\mathcal{D}$ and $B\subseteq A\Longrightarrow A\setminus B\in\mathcal{D}$
> 6. $\{A_n\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}$ and $A_1\subseteq A_2\subseteq \cdots\Longrightarrow\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_n\in\mathcal{D}$ 

^3a2a37

> [!thm] Dynkin system theorem
> Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a collection of subsets of $\Omega$ which is closed under pairwise intersection. If $\mathcal{D}$ is a Dynkin system containing $\mathcal{C}$, then $\mathcal{D}$ also contains the $\sigma$-field $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ generated by $\mathcal{C}$.

# The Consistency Theorem

> [!def] n-dimensional cylinder set 
> Let $\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}=\{\mu(x)\in\mathbb{R}:x\in[0,\infty)\}$. An n-dimensional cylinder set in $\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}$ is a set of the form
> $$
> C\triangleq\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}:(\mu(t_1),\cdots,\mu(t_n))\in A\}
> $$
> where $t_i\in[0,\infty),i=1,\cdots,n$ and $A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. $\mathcal{C}\triangleq$the field of all cylinder sets and $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)})\triangleq\sigma(\mathcal{C})$. 

> [!def] Finite-dimensional distributions and consistent
> Let $T\triangleq\{t=(t_1,\cdots,t_n):t_j\mbox{ are distinct }, t_j>0\}$. Suppose $t\in T$, we have a **probability measure $Q_t$** on $(\mathbb{R}^n,\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n))$. Then the family $\{Q_t\}_{t\in T}$  si called a family of finite-dimensional distributions.
> $\{Q_t\}_{t\in T}$ is said to be consistent provided that the following **two conditions** are satisfied:
> 1. If $\tilde{t}=(t_{i_1},\cdots,t_{i_n})$ is a permutation of $t=(t_1,\cdots,t_n)$, then for $\forall A_i\in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}),i=1,\cdots,n$, we have 
>    $$
>    Q_{t}(A_1\times A_2\times\cdots A_n)=Q_{\tilde{t}}(A_{i_1}\times A_{i_2}\times \cdots \times A_{t_n})
>    $$
> 2. If $t=(t_1,\cdots,t_n)$ and $s=(t_1,\cdots,t_{n-1})$ and $A\in\mathcal{R}^{n-1}$, then 
>    $$
>    Q_t(A\times\mathbb{R})=Q_{s}(A)
>    $$
>    
> If we have a **probability measure $\mathbb{P}$** on $(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}))$, then we can define a family of finite-dimensional distributions by
> $$
> Q_t(A)=\mathbb{P}[(\mu(t_1),\cdots,\mu(t_n))\in A]
> $$
> This family is easily seen to be consistent.

^c780ba

> [!thm] Daniell and Kolmogorov
> Let $\{Q_t\}_{t\in T}$ be a consistent family of finite-dimensional distributions. Then there is a probability measure $\mathbb{P}$ on $(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}))$, s.t. [[Kolmogorov's Construction of Brownian Motion#^c780ba|condition 2]] holds.

> [!note] Construction of Brownian Motion
> - **Goal**: construct a probability measure $\mathbb{P}$ on $(\Omega,\mathcal{F})=(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}))$ s.t. the process $B_t$ satisfies $B_t(\omega)\triangleq \mu(t)$, which is called **coordinate mapping process** and is **almost** a standard, one-dimensional Brownian motion under $\mathbb{P}$ 
> - For $0=s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_n$, the cumulative distribution function for $(B_{s_1},\cdots,B_{s_n})$ must be
>   $$
>   \begin{align}
> &F_{(s_1,\cdots,s_n)}(x_1,\cdots,x_n)\\
> =&\int_{-\infty}^{x_1}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{x_n}p(s_1,0,y_1)p(s_2-s_1,y_1,y_2)\cdots p(s_n-s_{n-1},y_{n-1},y_n){d}y_1\cdots dy_n
> \end{align}
>   $$
>   where $p$ is **Guassian Kernel**,
>   $$
>   p(t,x,y)\triangleq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^2}{2t}}
>   $$
> - We can **verify $F_{(s_1,\cdots,s_n)}(x_1,\cdots,x_n)$ is equivalent to $\{B_{s_j}-B_{s_{j-1}}\}_{j=1}^{n}$ is independent increments.** 
>   $\Longrightarrow$: We obtain the joint density function of $(B_{s_1},\cdots,B_{s_n})$ is 
>   $$
>   f_{(s_1,\cdots,s_n)}(x_1,\cdots,x_n)=p(s_1,0,x_1)\prod_{j=2}^{n}p(s_j-s_{j-1},x_{j-1},x_j)
>   $$
>   Let $(B_{s_1},\cdots,B_{s_n}-B_{s_n})=(Y_1,\cdots,Y_n)$. Note that 
>   $$
>   \begin{bmatrix}
> Y_1\\
> \vdots\\
> Y_n
> \end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
> 1 &0 &0 &\cdots &0\\
> -1&1 &0 &\cdots &0\\
> \vdots &\vdots &\vdots & &\vdots\\
> 0 &0 &\cdots &-1 &1
> \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
> B_{s_1}\\
> \vdots\\
> B_{s_n}
> \end{bmatrix}
>   $$
>   $$
>   \Longrightarrow
>   \begin{bmatrix}
> B_{s_1}\\
> \vdots\\
> B_{s_n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
> 1 &0 &0 &\cdots &0\\
> 1&1 &0 &\cdots &0\\
> \vdots &\vdots &\vdots & &\vdots\\
> 1 &1 &1 &\cdots &1
> \end{bmatrix}
>   \begin{bmatrix}
> Y_1\\
> \vdots\\
> Y_n
> \end{bmatrix}
>   $$
>   Then $g(y_1,\cdots,y_n)=f_{(s_1,\cdots,s_n)}(y_1,y_1+y_2\cdots,\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_i)$, that is 
>   $$
>   \begin{align}
> g(y_1,\cdots,y_n)&=p(s_1,0,y_1)\prod_{j=2}^{n}p(s_j-s_{j-1},\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}y_i,\sum_{i=1}^{j}y_{j})\\
> &=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s_1}}e^{-\frac{y_1^2}{2s_1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(s_j-s_{j-1})}}e^{-\frac{y_j^2}{2(s_j-s_{j-1})}}
> \end{align}
>   $$
>   Hence, $\{B_{s_j}-B_{s_{j-1}}\}_{j=1}^{n}$ are independent and $\sim N(0,s_j-s_{j-1})$. 
>   $\Longleftarrow$: Suppose $\{B_{s_j}-B_{s_{j-1}}\}_{j=1}^{n}$ are independent, the joint density is 
>   $$
>   g(y_1,\cdots,y_n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s_1}}e^{-\frac{y_1^2}{2s_1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(s_j-s_{j-1})}}e^{-\frac{y_j^2}{2(s_j-s_{j-1})}}
>   $$
>   By the linear transform, 
>   $$
>   f(x_1,\cdots,x_n)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi s_1}}e^{-\frac{x_1^2}{2s_1}}\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(s_j-s_{j-1})}}e^{-\frac{(x_j-x_{j-1})^2}{2(s_j-s_{j-1})}}
>   $$
> - Suppose if $t=(t_1,\cdots,t_n)$, every $t_j$ are not necessarily ordered but are distinct. For $A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, let $Q_t(A)$ be the probability under this distribution that $(B_{t_1}, \cdots , B_{t_n})$ is in $A$. This defines a family of finite-dimensional distributions ${Q_t}_{t\in T}$ which is **consistent**. Then the random vector $(B_{t_1}, \cdots , B_{t_n})$ have the distributions of $F$, where $t_j$ must be ordered from smallest to largest.

# The Kolmogorov-Centsov Theorem

> [!warning]
> We say the [[#^dd51a8|construction of Brownian motion]] is "almost", which implies we leave aside the requirement of sample path continuity for the moment and concentrate on the finite-dimensional distributions.

In this section, we complete the continuity of sample path of Brownian motion.

> [!thm] Kolmogorov-Centsov continuity theorem
> Suppose a process $X_t$ on a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ satisfies the condition
> $$
> \mathbb{E}|X_t-X_s|^\alpha\le C|t-s|^{1+\beta},0\le s,t\le T
> $$
> for some positive constants $\alpha,\beta$ and $C$. Then there exists a continuous modification $\tilde{X}_t$ of $X$, which is locally Hölder continuous with exponent $\gamma$ for every $\gamma\in(0,\frac{\beta}{\alpha})$
> $$
> \mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{0\le t-s<h(\omega)}\frac{|\tilde{X}_t(\omega)-\tilde{X}_s(\omega)|}{|t-s|^\gamma}\le \delta\right]=1
> $$
> where $h(\omega)$ is an $a.s.$ positive random variable and $\delta > 0$ is an appropriate constant.

^5a608f

**Proof**
Without loss of generality, let $T=1$. By Chebyshev's inequality, for $\varepsilon>0$, we have 
$$
\mathbb{P}[|X_t-X_s|\ge \varepsilon]\le\frac{\mathbb{E}|X_t-X_s|^\alpha}{\varepsilon^\alpha}\le C\varepsilon^{-\alpha}|t-s|^{1+\beta}
$$
Then as $s\to t$, we have $X_s\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}X_t$.
Take $t=\frac{k}{2^n},s=\frac{k-1}{2^n}$ and $\varepsilon=2^{-\gamma n}$, by preceding inequality, we obtain
$$
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{1\le k\le 2^n}|X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}-X_{\frac{k-1}{2^n}}|>2^{-\gamma n}\right]&=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcup_{1\le k\le 2^n}\{|X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}-X_{\frac{k-1}{2^n}}|>2^{-\gamma n}\}\right]\\
&\le \sum_{k=1}^{2^n}\mathbb{P}\left[|X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}-X_{\frac{k-1}{2^n}}|>2^{-\gamma n}\right]\\
&\le \sum_{k=1}^{2^n}C2^{-\gamma\alpha n}2^{-n(1+\beta)}=C2^{-n(\beta-\alpha\gamma)}
\end{align}
$$
Since $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n(\beta-\alpha\gamma)}<\infty$, by Borel-Cantelli lemma, there exists a set $\Omega^*$ s.t. $\mathbb{P}(\Omega^*)=1$ and for $\omega\in\Omega^*$, we have 
$$
\max_{1\le k\le 2^n}|X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}-X_{\frac{k-1}{2^n}}|\le 2^{-\gamma n},\forall n\ge n^*(\omega)
$$
**Claim:** $X$ is uniformly $\gamma$-Hölder continuous on $D=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D_n=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/2^n\cap[0,1])$ i.e. there exists $\delta=\delta(\omega)$ s.t.  ^8ee428
$$
|X_t-X_s|\le \delta|t-s|^{\gamma},t,s\in D
$$
Now we prove the claim. Let $t\in[\frac{k}{2^{n}},\frac{k+1}{2^{n}}]\cap D,k=0,1,\cdots,2^n$. Then there exists a sequence $\{p_n\}$ s.t. 
$$
\frac{k}{2^{n}}=\frac{p_n}{2^n},\frac{p_{n+1}}{2^{n+1}},\cdots,\frac{p_{N}}{2^N}=t,|\frac{p_m}{2^m}-\frac{p_{m+1}}{2^{m+1}}|=\frac{1}{2^{m+1}},n\le m<N
$$
Then we have 
$$
|X_t-X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}|\le \sum_{m=n}^{N-1}|X_{\frac{p_m}{2^m}}-X_{\frac{p_{m+1}}{2^{m+1}}}|<\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}2^{-\gamma m}=\frac{2^{-\gamma n}}{1-2^{-\gamma}}
$$
It implies $X_t,t\in D$ is bounded on $D$. Let $\delta_0=\sup_{t\in D}X_t$. For $\frac{k-1}{2^n}\le s<\frac{k}{2^n}<t\le \frac{k+1}{2^n}$, and such $n$ satisfies $\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}<|t-s|<\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}$. 
**Cases1: $n<n^*(\omega)$.** Since $|t-s|\ge 2^{-n^*}$, we have
$$
\frac{|X_t-X_s|}{|t-s|^\gamma}\le\frac{2\delta_0}{2^{-n^*\gamma}}\triangleq \delta_1(\omega)
$$
**Cases2: $n\ge n^*$** We have 
$$
\begin{align}
|X_t-X_s|&\le|X_t-X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}|+|X_{\frac{k}{2^n}}-X_s|\le \frac{2^{-\gamma n+1}}{1-2^{-\gamma}}\\
&=\frac{2}{1-2^{-\gamma}}(2^{-(n+1)}2)^\gamma\le\frac{2}{1-2^{-\gamma}}(2|t-s|)^\gamma\triangleq\delta_2(\omega)|t-s|^\gamma 
\end{align} 
$$
Let $\delta=\delta_1\vee\delta_2$. Hence, $|X_t-X_s|\le\delta|t-s|^\gamma$ for $s,t\in D$.
After proving the claim, we can define 
$$
\tilde{X}_t=\begin{cases}
X_t,&t\in D\\
\lim_{t_m\to t}X_{t_m},&t\notin D
\end{cases}
$$
where $\{t_m\}\subseteq D$ since $D$ is dense in $[0,1]$. By the uniform $\gamma$-Hölder continuity, the limit is independent of $t_m$, and the resulting $\tilde{X}_t$ is also $\gamma$-Hölder continuous. 
**QED**
We also introduce the high dimensional form of Kolmogorov-Centsov theorem.

> [!thm]
> A **random field** is a collection of random variables $\{X_t; t \in \mathcal{A}\}$, where $\mathcal{A}$ is a partially ordered set. Suppose $\{X_t; t \in [0, T]^d\}, d \geq 2$, is a random field satisfying
> 
> $$
> \mathbb{E}|X_t - X_s|^\alpha \leq C \|t - s\|^{d+\beta}
> $$
> 
> for some positive constants $\alpha, \beta$, and $C$. Then $X_t$ have a continuous modification $\tilde{X}_t$ and satisfies
> 
> $$
> P\left[\omega: \sup_{0 < \|t-s\| < h(\omega)} \frac{|\tilde{X}_t(\omega) - \tilde{X}_s(\omega)|}{\|t-s\|^\gamma} \leq \delta\right] = 1.
> $$

> [!proposition]
> If $B_t - B_s, 0 < s < t$, is normally distributed with mean zero and variance $t - s$, then for each positive integer $n$, there is a positive constant $C_{n}$ for which
> $$
> \mathbb{E}|B_t-B_s|^{2n}=C_n|t-s|^n
> $$

^0ee882

**Proof**
In fact, $C_n$ is the $2n$-th moment of normal distribution., that is $C_n=(2n-1)!!$. 
**QED**

> [!thm]
> There is a probability measure $\mathbb{P}$ on $(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}))$, and a stochastic process $W_t$ on the same space, such that under $\mathbb{P}$, $W$ is a Brownian motion.

**Proof**
By [[#^5a608f|Kolmogorov-Centsov continuity theorem]] and [[#^0ee882|Bronian motion satisfies the condition]], we obtain for $T>0$, there exists a continuous modification $W^T$ of the process $B$. Let 
$$
\Omega_T=\{\omega:W^T_t(\omega)=B_t(\omega),t\in[0,T]\cap\mathbb{Q}\},\Omega_0=\bigcap_{T=1}^{\infty}\Omega_T
$$
so $\mathbb{P}(\Omega_T)=1$. For $T_1,T_2$ are distinct, 
$$
W^{T_1}=W^{T_2},t\in[0,T_1\wedge T_2]\cap\mathbb{Q}
$$
Since both processes are continuous on $[0, T_1\wedge T_2]$, we must have 
$$
W^{T_1}_t(\omega)=W^{T_2}_t(\omega),(t,\omega)\in([0,T_1\wedge T_2],\Omega_0)
$$
Define $W_t(\omega)$ to be this common value. For $\omega\in\Omega_0$, set $W_t(\omega) = 0$ for all $t > 0$.
**QED**

> [!question]
> Let $X = \{X_t; 0 \leq t < \infty\}$ be a stochastic process for which  
> $X_0, X_{t_1} - X_{t_0}, \ldots, X_{t_n} - X_{t_{n-1}}$ are independent random variables, for every  
> integer $n \geq 1$ and indices $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_n < \infty$. Then for any fixed  
> $0 \leq s < t < \infty$, the increment $X_t - X_s$ is independent of $\mathcal{F}_s^X$.

^a01520

> [!done]
> For $u\le s$, we take the partition of $[0,u]:0=u_0<u_1<\cdots<u_n=u$. For $X_u$, we have 
> $$
> X_u=X_{u_0}+X_{u_1}-X_{u_0}+\cdots+X_{u_n}-X_{u_{n-1}}
> $$
> Since $X_t-X_s$ is independent with $X_{u_0},X_{u_1}-X_{u_0},\cdots,X_{u_{n}}-X_{u_{n-1}}$, $X_t-X_s$ is independent with $X_u$ for $\forall u\le s$. The $\sigma$-algebra$\sigma(X_0,X_{s_1},\cdots,X_{s_n})=\sigma(X_0,X_{s_1}-X_0,\cdots,X_{s_n}-X_{s_{n-1}})$. Then let a collection
> $$
> \mathscr{C}=\bigcup\sigma(X_0,X_{s_1},\cdots,X_{s_n})
> $$
> $\mathscr{C}$ is independent of $X_t-X_s$ and is closed under finite intersection. Let 
> $$
> \mathscr{D}=\{A\in\mathcal{F}^X_t:A\bot X_t-X_s\}
> $$
> Note that $\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{D}$ and by [[#^273f1e|Dynkin system theorem]], $\mathcal{F}^X_s=\sigma(\mathscr{C})\subseteq\mathscr{D}\Longrightarrow\mathscr{D}=\mathcal{F}^X_s$.

> [!question]
> Show that the only $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0, \infty)})$-measurable set contained in $C[0, \infty)$ is the empty set. 

> [!done]
> Suppose that $\emptyset\ne E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)})$ and $E\subseteq C[0,\infty)$. $E$ has the form 
> $$
> E=\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}:(\omega(t_1),\omega(t_2),\cdots)\in A\}
> $$
> Take $s\in [0,\infty)\setminus\{t_1,t_2,\cdots\}$. We define the function $\mu:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$:
> $$
> \mu(t)=\begin{cases}
> \omega(t),&t\ne s\\
> \omega(s)+1,&t=s
> \end{cases}
> $$
> Note that $\mu(t)$ is not continuous on $t=s$ and thus $\mu(t)\notin C[0,\infty)$. For $t\in \{t_1,t_2,\cdots\},s\notin \{t_1,t_2,\cdots\}$, we have $\mu(t)=\omega(t)$. Then $(\mu(t))_{t\in\{t_1,t_2,\cdots\}}\in A$ and thus $\mu\in E\subseteq C[0,\infty)$, it is a contraction! Hence, $E=\emptyset$.
